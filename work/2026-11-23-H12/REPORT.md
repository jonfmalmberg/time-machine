## What is in this folder
- `defs.add.json`: eight new definition entries, one per line (window, sound, markers, fits, near).
- `playlists.add.json`: eight new playlist entries, one per line (status, intro, peak, albums, fold, note, yrs).
- `sizes.json`: genre id → low, high, core, basis, confidence.
- `sources.json`: genre id → list of claims (kind def/size/fit/near/song/album, artist, title, url, quote, read, optional note), in the shape `tools/check_evidence.py` reads.
- `unverified.json`: genre id → `alias` (title used → spelling on the page) and `unverified` (recalled or half-seen, not confirmed; fetch failures).
- `evidence-check.json`: this run's output of `tools/check_evidence.py` over `sources.json`.
- `notes/<genre id>.md`: each sub-agent's full notes: (a) confidence, (b) every source address, (c) neighbours with no id, (d) what fell short. The report below condenses them.
- `brief.md`: the brief every sub-agent was given.
- `REPORT.md`: this file.

### Confidence, per genre
**`dohori`.** Medium confidence in the definition and that each song exists and is a lok dohori duet, low in the list being what an insider would choose and in the size. The definition rests on English and Nepali Wikipedia, Anna Stirr's OUP blog post (madal, improvised couplets, metre and end rhyme), Nepali Times (rodhi ghar origin, 30-second repartee) and the free part of a Himal Southasian article. No source gives a start year; the tree's 1800 is untested. 16 of the 26 songs are tied only by a title naming the genre ("Dohori Battle", iTunes albums called "Nepali Lok Dohori"); the others by a page calling the song or the singer dohori. Weakest: Prem Raja Mahat and Rekha Shah's "Simsime Panima" (1996), tied only by Mahat's Wikipedia category "Dohori singers", and the only song before 2008. The list is commercial studio duets of 2008–2024; there is no village or rodhi ghar dohori. iTunes years may be digital-release dates. Only seven titles are in Devanagari (where a Nepali page printed them); the rest keep the page's romanisation.

**`tamangselo`.** Medium confidence in the definition and that the songs exist, low in each being a typical Tamang selo and in the size. The definition rests on English Wikipedia (Tamang Selo, Tamang people, Damphu, Music of Nepal, Music of Sikkim, Hira Devi Waiba) and a Nepal Traveller feature. No page dates the start; the window names the first recordings found (Radio Nepal 1966, HMV 1974 and 1978). All 26 songs come from iTunes lookup JSON. 20 are tied only by a track or album title naming the genre, and at least five of those are instrumentals. Three are tied by a page (Kathmandu Post on Kunti Moktan's song, a "Damphu Geet" label, a Tamang Samaj directory). Two rest only on the singer's Wikipedia genre (Hira Devi Waiba, Navneet Aditya Waiba). Hira Devi Waiba's first recorded selo ("Chura ta Hoina Astura") was not found on a readable page. Titles are romanised as on the pages; no page gave Devanagari.

**`pasillo`.** Medium-to-good confidence in the songs and definition, low in the size. Definition from Spanish Wikipedia (Pasillo (música), Pasillo ecuatoriano) and English Pasillo. All recordings from iTunes lookup JSON (Ecuador and Colombia stores). 21 recordings are Ecuadorian, 9 Colombian. Twelve songs are named pasillos by their own or a genre page, twelve by a performer or composer page, two by "(Pasillo Ecuador)" in the track title, and two only by the album title (Julio Jaramillo's "Náufrago de amor", Olimpo Cárdenas's "Hacia el calvario"). Weakest: "Pescador, lucero y río" and "Pueblito viejo" are called pasillos only by English Wikipedia; Colombian sources may call them bambucos. Most years are compilation or reissue years; the Dúo Ecuador takes are null and Olimpo Cárdenas carries 1996 on a ℗ 2006 album. Venezuela, Panama and Central America have no recordings.

**`meringue`.** Medium-to-good confidence in the songs and definition, low in the size. Definition from English and French Wikipedia and Harold Courlander's notes to Folkways FW 6837, which separate Haitian méringue from the Dominican dance and name its salon, dance and carnival kinds. Fifteen songs are marked "Meringue" on a label or in the French Wikipedia discography of Issa El-Saieh (four also on Internet Archive 78 labels); eight by a track title naming the genre (Duroseau's piano pieces and others); two by a Wikipedia page ("Choucoune", Lamothe's "Nibo"); seven only by an album title (Jazz Majestic Orchestra, Ti Band L'Avenir, Edner Guignard, Emy de Pradines). The list covers about 1936–1956 plus later salon recordings; no page ties any 1960s–80s orchestra track (Jazz des Jeunes, Septentrional) to méringue. The 1936–37 Lomax recordings carry their 2009 issue year.

**`gaitazuliana`.** Medium-to-good confidence in the definition and songs, low in the size. Definition from English and Spanish Wikipedia (furro, cuatro, charrasca, tambora; 6/8; copla and chorus; the season from 18 November; gaita de tambora; first recordings in the late 1940s). Every song is on an iTunes lookup track list, and every performer is tied by a page (Ricardo Aguirre, Gran Coquivacoa, Maracaibo 15, Guaco, and the groups in English Wikipedia's list of famous gaita groups); three also by title. Ties are by performer, not track by track; weakest for Maracaibo 15, which also plays parrandas. Many years come from Discomoda and Foca compilations or digital reissues; five Ricardo Aguirre years are null. No Saladillo track alone, no 1940s 78s and nothing after the 1990s. No MusicBrainz request was made.

**`pasodoble`.** Good confidence in the definition and songs, low in the size. All recordings from iTunes lookup JSON (Spain store); every genre statement from Spanish Wikipedia. Twenty-one songs have a Wikipedia article on the piece calling it a pasodoble; five more are in the main article's list of best-known pasodobles; every band track is also on an album titled with the genre. "Cocidito madrileño" (Pepe Blanco) is tied by title only. Debatable: "Y viva España" (a Belgian song of 1971 in pasodoble style) and "Los nardos" (pasacalle or pasodoble, from a revista). All band recordings are modern (1995–2020); no period band 78 was found. Ten sung tracks have null release years because they sit on reissue compilations; their years written come from Wikipedia.

**`harana`.** Medium confidence in the definition and songs, low in the size. Definition from English Wikipedia (Harana, Kundiman) and an Asian Journal feature quoting guitarist Florante Aguilar on the 2/4 habanera rhythm, guitar introduction and archaic lyrics. Every song is on an album whose title names the genre (Introducing the Harana Kings, Ruben Tagalog's Harana, Aguilar's The Art of Harana, Charmaine Clamor's My Harana), and all but Clamor's "Harana sa Dilim" also have a page calling the performer a harana singer. Weak points: Ruben Tagalog's ten tracks are from a ℗ 2006 Villar collection and have null years; Aguilar's three tracks are guitar-only; some songs ("Maruja", "Paraluman", "Dalagang Pilipina") may be danzas or kundiman sung at a serenade. No Visayan or Ilocano haranas, no women singers, nothing dated before about 1950.

**`jiangnansizhu`.** Medium confidence in the definition and songs, low in the size. Definition from English Wikipedia (region, Shanghai clubs, erhu, dizi or xiao, pipa, yangqin; two to ten players; the Eight Great Pieces; teahouse amateurs) and Chinese Wikipedia 江南丝竹 (in vogue from 1860, heterophony, 你繁我簡). Tracks from MusicBrainz and iTunes lookup JSON. Six songs are tied by a performer page (the Lu Chunling, Ma Shenglong, Zhou Hui and Zhou Hao quartet, whose 1983 cassette is titled 三六（江南丝竹）); fourteen by an album title naming the genre; one, the Huxing Ting Teahouse Ensemble's "Zhonghua Liuban" (PAN, 1994), by the piece being one of the Eight Great Pieces and the album being called Sizhu. Years are edition years (Naxos 1992–93 recordings dated 2002; the 2001 "中国地方音乐大系" set). iTunes dates the quartet's album 1970, MusicBrainz 1983; 1983 was used. No pre-1949 recordings. The `guoyue` near-miss matches "China Broadcast National Orchestra" to Wikipedia's orchestra by name only.

## What fell short
- **Genre ties by title.** Dohori (16 of 26) and Tamang selo (20 of 26) are mostly tied by a track or album title naming the genre. Harana and Jiangnan sizhu rest largely on album titles; méringue has seven album-title-only songs. No liner notes were read except one Folkways PDF; FP 8, FW 6833, FW 6811 and FW 6824 have no text layer.
- **Missing layers.** Dohori has no improvised village or rodhi ghar recordings and one song before 2008. Tamang selo lacks its first recording (Hira Devi Waiba, "Chura ta Hoina Astura") and the cassette-era core. Pasillo has no Venezuelan, Panamanian or Central American recordings. Méringue has nothing from the 1960s–80s orchestras and no Lumane Casimir. Gaita zuliana has no 1940s 78s, no Saladillo alone and nothing after the 1990s. Pasodoble has no period band recordings (the 1926 España cañí on Odeon is named but not found). Harana has no Visayan (Stax Huguete) or Ilocano (Felipe Alonzo) originals and no women singers. Jiangnan sizhu has no 78 rpm or 1950s–60s club recordings.
- **Years.** 33 of 211 songs have a null release year (pasodoble 10, harana 10, gaita zuliana 5, méringue 3, pasillo 2, one each in the others). Many years are compilation, reissue, digital-release or edition years rather than first issues; details are in each `notes/<id>.md`, section (a).
- **Start dates.** No page dates the start of dohori (tree 1800) or Tamang selo (tree 1800); harana's 1850 rests on "believed to have started in the 1800s"; pasillo's 1830 fits "las primeras décadas del siglo XIX" only roughly.
- **Unreachable pages.** Anna Stirr's study and the full Himal article (bot check, paywall); the Tamang Online article and the Wayback Machine; the Ekantipur book article (body loads by script); Inquirer pages on the Harana Kings (short shell); Spanish Wikipedia pages that do not exist (Espumas, La gata golosa, Pueblito viejo, Enrique Ibáñez Mora, Dueto de Antaño). MusicBrainz was used sparingly on purpose (eight researchers at once); a 503 at the start of the run was not retried in a loop.
- **Playability.** Streaming availability was not checked for any genre.

## Questions for the lead
1. **Jiangnan sizhu parentage.** `data/parentage.json` has `kunqu` → `jiangnansizhu` (principal) and `guqin` → `jiangnansizhu` (other). Chinese Wikipedia says the Eight Great Pieces share no tunes with kunqu and the genre is closer to shifan luogu (十番锣鼓); no page read supports the guqin line. English Wikipedia says the modern Chinese orchestra "first developed out of Jiangnan sizhu ensemble in the 1920s", which would support a `jiangnansizhu` → `guoyue` line the file lacks. Nothing was changed.
2. **梵王宫 / 梵王工.** The iTunes page prints the title 梵王工; the playlist uses 梵王宫 as the standard title, with the page spelling as alias. The sub-agent standardised the character; the lead may prefer to keep the page form until a second source confirms it.
3. **Pasillo near-misses.** `bambuco` and `valscriollo` were used. The natural Ecuadorian neighbours (bolero/rockola, sanjuanito, albazo, yaraví) have no id. Two playlist songs ("Pescador, lucero y río", "Pueblito viejo") may be bambucos by other sources; the lead may want to check them at the master-table step.
4. **Gaita zuliana near-miss under `cumbia`.** Colombian gaita (the gaita-flute music of San Jacinto) has no id; the near entry for Los Gaiteros de San Jacinto uses `cumbia`, because English Wikipedia calls them a cumbia group and the `cumbia` definition names gaita flutes. If the lead thinks that stretches `cumbia`, drop the entry.
