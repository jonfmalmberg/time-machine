# dohori: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`dohori`.** I am fairly sure of the definition and that every listed song exists and is a lok dohori duet; I am much less sure that the list is the one an insider would draw up, and of the size. All 84 claims are `found`. The definition rests on English Wikipedia (Dohori, Music of Nepal), Nepali Wikipedia (दोहोरी गीत), Anna Stirr's OUP blog post on festival dohori (madal, improvised couplets, meter and end rhyme, a recent hit's tune), Nepali Times (2019: Gurung rodi ghar origin, 30-second repartee, Tij songs) and the free part of a Himal Southasian article (2011: rodhi ghar, dohori sanjh restaurants, backbone of the recording industry). No source gives a start year, so the tree's 1800 is untested. The instruments (madal, harmonium, sarangi) come from two singers' Wikipedia pages and the OUP post, not from a general description of recorded dohori; bansuri was left out for that reason.

Song ties:
- **The title or album names the genre** (16 songs): Dohori Battle, Dohori Battle 2, Dohori Ko Raja, Roila Dohori, Jhim Jhim Sanu Dohori, Dami Dekhinchha (Live Dohori), Pachheuri Leu Baina Live Dohori 2, and the iTunes albums titled "Nepali Lok Dohori", "Lok Dohori" or "Charchit Lok Dohori Song" (Parewa Ko Jodi, Karnali Ko Chhal, Maya Numberi, Gayal Mutu, Pare Sano Jaat, Siriri Lau Hawa Chalyo, Budhi Ramro Arkako, Rodhi Ghar, Sacho Maya Maile Gardani). These ties are by title only.
- **A page calls the song or singer dohori**: सालको पातको टपरी हुनी (Nepali Wikipedia: most viewed lok dohori song); Banki Chari Banaima Ramayo (English Wikipedia: "2019 Lok Dohori song"); Bishnu Majhi's songs (Nepali Wikipedia credits her to lok dohori; Phul Ramro Gulabko's cited video is titled "New Nepali Lok Dohori"); Bola Maya and गल्बन्दि through Shanti Shree Pariyar ("लोक दोहोरी गायिका"); Badri Pangeni ("लोक तथा दोहोरी गायक").
- **Weakest**: Prem Raja Mahat & Rekha Shah "Simsime Panima" (1996) is tied only by Mahat's Wikipedia category "Dohori singers"; it is the only pre-2008 song, so the radio and cassette era (1978-2000) is thin. Badri Pangeni's song has no year. iTunes years are digital release dates and may postdate first issue. The commercial studio duet dominates; improvised village dohori has no recording here.

The size is a judgement from singers' output and the academy's membership, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Rodhi / rodhi ghar songs** (Gurung courting-house singing), the village root of dohori; no id.
- **Deuda** (far-west Nepal circle-dance song duels) and **hakpare** (eastern hills); Himal names both as sibling duet forms; no id.
- **Teej (Tij) songs**, the women's festival songs now largely released as lok dohori; no id.
- **Jat-jatin** (Mithila duet form, Tarai); no id.
- **Lok pop / Nepali folk pop** (folk tunes with pop production, e.g. later Prakash Saput); no id.
- **Kathe git** (Kathmandu-area festival dohori style named by Stirr); no id.

## (d) What fell short
- No recording of improvised village or rodhi ghar dohori: none on a page a script can read.
- Before 2008 only one song (1996). Radio Nepal recordings of the 1970s-80s and the 1990s cassette hits (Khola Pari Nirmaya 1986, Paanko Paat 1988) are named on pages but without a dohori tie for the song.
- Devanagari titles only where a Nepali page prints them; the rest are romanised as on the page.
- MusicBrainz holds almost nothing for the genre (one release found, not read); iTunes years may be reissue dates.
- Anna Stirr's study and the full Himal article could not be read (bot check, paywall).
