# gaitazuliana: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`gaitazuliana`.** Fairly sure of the definition and the songs, unsure of the size. All 98 claims are `found`. The definition rests on English and Spanish Wikipedia (instruments, furro as bass, 6/8, copla and chorus, the season from 18 November, the tamborera subgenre of the early 1970s, first recordings in the late 1940s). Every song is on a raw iTunes lookup track list, and every performer is tied to the genre by a page:
- **Performer page.** Ricardo Aguirre (es.wikipedia: "El Monumental de la Gaita"; en.wikipedia names him), Gran Coquivacoa ("agrupación musical de gaita zuliana"), Maracaibo 15 ("agrupación gaitera"), Guaco (es.wikipedia: its first records were gaita zuliana, "un grupo de gaita tradicional"), and Cardenales del Éxito, Rincón Morales, Estrellas del Zulia and Barrio Obrero (named in en.wikipedia's list of famous gaita groups).
- **Title also names the genre.** Estrellas del Zulia's "Gaita zuliana", Barrio Obrero's album *La máxima expresión de la gaita zuliana*, Guaco's *Gaita a todo color*.

**Weak ties:**
- Most tracks come from Discomoda and Foca compilations or digital reissues, so several years are the dates on those pages, not first-release dates. Five Ricardo Aguirre tracks have null years; "La grey zuliana" uses 1968 from es.wikipedia (recorded with Saladillo).
- Maracaibo 15 mixes gaita with parrandas and other forms, so its two tracks are tied only by the group page and its list of hits, not by a page saying each is a gaita.
- Guaco's 1973 tracks belong to its gaita period by the page's account; the band later became a salsa-pop orchestra.
- The 1967 Estrellas del Zulia and 1976 Gran Coquivacoa dates come from 2014/2022 digital reissues.

The size is a judgement made before collecting, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Colombian gaita (música de gaitas, the gaita-flute music of San Jacinto).** No id; the near entry uses `cumbia`, whose definition already names gaita flutes and which en.wikipedia uses for Los Gaiteros de San Jacinto.
- **Venezuelan parranda and aguinaldo** (the other Christmas song forms, which Maracaibo 15 also plays). No id.
- **Gaita de tambora and chimbangueles** (the Afro-Zulian drum traditions of the south of the lake). No id; `wadrum` is the tree's broad parent line.
- **Trinidad parang** (en.wikipedia says Trinidad and Tobago adopted gaita as parang). No id.

## (d) What fell short
- No MusicBrainz request was made: iTunes and Wikipedia covered everything, and a few MusicBrainz releases would add little.
- No song of Saladillo alone, and no early 78s (Gaiteros del Zulia, late 1940s), were found on a readable page.
- Neguito Borjas' best-known Gran Coquivacoa gaitas ("Sin rencor", the Gaita del Año winners of 1986-89) were not on the track lists read.
- Years are thin: many come from compilation or reissue pages, and five are null.
- Newer gaita (1990s onward: Guaco's later work, Melody Gaita, Koquimba, Los Chiquinquireños) is not represented.
