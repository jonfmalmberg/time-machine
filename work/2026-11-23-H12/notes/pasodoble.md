# pasodoble: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`pasodoble`.** I am sure of the songs and the definition, and much less sure of the size. All 97 claims are `found`. Every recording was read from the raw iTunes lookup JSON (country=es). Every genre statement was read from raw Spanish Wikipedia HTML. Each of the 27 songs is tied to the pasodoble in one or more of three ways:
- **A Spanish Wikipedia article on the piece calls it a pasodoble.** This covers 21 songs: España cañí, Paquito el chocolatero, La Gracia de Dios, Suspiros de España, Amparito Roca, Ragón Falez, La Entrada, El fallero, Pasodoble Islas Canarias, El relicario, En tierra extraña, La morena de mi copla, Mi jaca, Francisco Alegre, Campanera, Madrecita María del Carmen, No te vayas de Navarra, De Andalucía yo soy, Los nardos ("a ritmo de pasacalle o pasodoble"), Y viva España ("estilo pasodoble") and Cántame un pasodoble español, whose title names the genre. El gato montés is covered too: its article says the opera "contiene un popular pasodoble".
- **The main Pasodoble article names the piece in its list of best-known pasodobles.** This covers El abanico, Valencia, Agüero, Tercio de quites and El gato montés.
- **The album or track title names the genre.** This covers every band track: *El Pasodoble*, *Pasodobles Taurinos*, *Pasodobles Toreros*, *Pasodobles, Vol. 2* and *Pasodobles de Villena*. It also covers Angelillo and Joselito, on *Pasodobles Cantados por Sus Creadores*, and Juanita Reina, on *20 Pasodobles Cantados*. **Pepe Blanco's "Cocidito madrileño" is tied by title only**: the track is "(Pasodoble)" and the album is *Pasodobles*.

**Weak ties and gaps:**
- **Y viva España** is a Belgian song from 1971 in pasodoble style. It is in the list because Manolo Escobar's Spanish version is a canonical pasodoble hit, but it is the most debatable entry.
- **Los nardos** is described as pasacalle or pasodoble. It comes from a revista (variety show), not the bullring.
- **The band recordings are modern** (1995–2020) performances of old pieces. No period band 78 is on the list.
- **Most release years for the sung tracks are null**, because iTunes gives reissue dates. The years written come from Wikipedia.
- **Two release years come from iTunes.** Celia Gámez's 1939 (℗ 1939 Odeon) and Lolita Sevilla's 1958 single are taken from iTunes.

**The size is a judgement** from the number of whole albums seen, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Copla / canción española (tonadilla, 1920s–1970s).** The sung pasodoble canción belongs half to this form. Examples: Concha Piquer, Estrellita Castro, Juanita Reina, Lola Flores, Manolo Escobar. The tree's `iberianpop` ("romance, copla, décima") ends in 1800, so it does not fit and was not stretched. Pieces from the copla repertoire that are not pasodobles (for example Concha Piquer's "Tatuaje" or "Ojos verdes") would be the natural near-misses if a copla id existed.
- **Spanish military march (marcha militar, himno).** Pieces such as "Soldadito español" and "Banderita" are close; `brass` (Central European brass bands, ended 1930) is the parent, not a near-miss.
- **Música festera / marcha mora of Moros y Cristianos.** The slow "marcha mora" and "marcha cristiana" played by the same Levante bands (Villena, Alcoy) as the festero pasodoble have no id.
- **Ballroom / DanceSport pasodoble.** This is the competition Latin dance on España cañí-type music and has no id.
- **Latin American pasodoble** (Mexican pasodoble taurino, the Ecuadorian and Puerto Rican pasodoble). No id; not researched.
- **Revista musical / chotis madrileño** (Las leandras, "Los nardos"). No id; `zarzuela` is the nearest.

## (d) What fell short
- **No period recordings of the band pasodoble.** The first España cañí recordings (Banda del Regimiento de Ingenieros de Madrid, Odeon and Regal Zonophone, 1926) and Marcos Redondo's first sung version (1931) are named on Wikipedia, but no readable track page was found. MusicBrainz was not tried, to keep requests to that shared service low; a MusicBrainz search for 1920s Spanish band 78s is the next step.
- **No flamenco near-miss.** A Paco de Lucía rumba would have been the obvious contrast (`rumbaflam`), but the English Wikipedia "Entre dos aguas" page had no article body, so it was dropped.
- **La Entrada's year is unclear.** The same Wikipedia article gives both 1925 and 1922 for the premiere, so it is left null.
- **Not researched:** Lola Flores, Rocío Jurado, Antonio Molina and Juanito Valderrama as pasodoble singers (all are on the read compilations); the Valencian festero repertoire beyond El fallero and Paquito; the tuna (student band) pasodoble; Agustín Lara's pasodobles.
- **The size** is not based on any catalogue count.
