# meringue: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`meringue`.** I am fairly sure of the songs and the definition, and unsure of the size. All 78 claims are `found`. The definition rests on English Wikipedia (Méringue, Dance in Haiti, Music of Haiti), French Wikipedia (Méringue) and Harold Courlander's liner notes for Folkways FW 6837 (PDF with a text layer). Those notes set the Haitian méringue apart from the Dominican dance and name its three kinds: salon, dance and carnival. Every one of the 31 songs is tied to méringue in one of these ways:
- **A label or discography marks the side "Meringue".** This covers 15 songs: the Issa El-Saieh 78s and the related Durosier, Sicot and Bebo Valdés sides in French Wikipedia's discography, which also carries 1947–1956 years. Four of them are confirmed again by Internet Archive 78 labels. Ernest Lamy's Seeco 78 is marked "Merengue Haitiano; Grabado en Haiti", and the Gilles Sala / Léardée 78 is marked "Méringue Haïtienne".
- **The track title names the genre.** This covers 8 songs: Duroseau's four "(meringue)" pieces, "Instrumental Meringue", Destiné's "Meringue", "Élégie-Meringue" and "Meringue Frangeul".
- **Only the album title names the genre.** This is the weakest tie and covers 7 songs: the Jazz Majestic Orchestra (Folkways *Calypso and Meringues*, backed by Courlander's note pointing to FP 8 for "other examples of Haitian Meringues"), Ti Band L'Avenir (Corason *Méringue*), Edner Guignard (*Les Belles Méringues d'Haïti*) and Emy de Pradines (*Original Meringues*).
- **A Wikipedia page calls the song a méringue.** This covers 2 songs. Ti zwazo is Choucoune, called a méringue on English and French Wikipedia; it also has the album-title tie. Lamothe's "Nibo" is named by French Wikipedia as one of his slow méringue airs.

**Weak ties and gaps:**
- The 1960s–80s orchestras (Jazz des Jeunes, Septentrional) are missing because no page ties any of their tracks to méringue.
- Edner Guignard, Ernest Lamy and Gilles Sala have no year.
- The size is a judgement, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Contredanse / kontradans and carabinier.** These are the 19th-century Haitian dances that méringue grew out of, according to English Wikipedia. The Frémeaux set has "Contre danse" tracks.
- **Vodou jazz.** This is the 1940s–60s orchestra style (Jazz des Jeunes) that put Vodou rhythms into méringue and dance-band music. It sits between `meringue`, `kompa` and `mizikrasin`, and none of those fits it well.
- **Cadence rampa (Wébert Sicot).** English Wikipedia calls it Sicot's own name for modern méringue. The nearest id is `kompa`, but the two are not the same.
- **Haitian art music (piano and guitar méringues by Lamothe, Élie, Jeanty, Saintonge and Casséus).** No classical-Caribbean id exists, so these are kept here as salon méringue.

Dominican merengue (`merengue`) and konpa (`kompa`) have ids and are used for the two near-misses. Twoubadou (`twoubadou`) is not used as a near-miss, because English Wikipedia describes its archetype singer as a singer of topical merengs, so no clean near-miss was found.

## (d) What fell short
- **No 1960s–1980s méringue band recordings.** No page read ties Jazz des Jeunes, Septentrional or Ensemble Citadelle tracks to méringue. Researching Haitian discography sites (for example haitimusic or konpa.info, not read) or liner notes of the Ostinato and Frémeaux sets might fix this. Frémeaux's own site search returned no product page.
- **Lumane Casimir:** French Wikipedia calls her the queen of méringue, but no recording by her was found.
- **Lomax tracks:** only the 2009 issue year is given, because the 1936–37 recording dates were not read per track.
- **Folkways scans:** the FP 8 (Calypso and Meringues), FW 6833, FW 6811 and FW 6824 liner-note PDFs have no text layer, so only FW 6837 could be quoted.
- **Composed works without recordings:** Jeanty's "Zizipan", Élie's "Méringue populaire" and Lamothe's "La Dangereuse" are named as méringues, but no recording of them was read.
- **Archive titles:** Internet Archive's 78 search was used only for its titles. Each 78 page used was then fetched and quoted.
- **The size** is not based on any catalogue count.
