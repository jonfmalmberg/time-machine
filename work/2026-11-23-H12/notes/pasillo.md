# pasillo: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`pasillo`.** I am fairly sure of the songs and the definition, and unsure of the size. All 91 claims are `found`. Every recording was read from the raw iTunes lookup JSON (Ecuador or Colombia store), and every genre tie from Spanish or English Wikipedia or from a title that names the genre. The list covers both branches: 21 recordings by Ecuadorian performers and 9 by Colombian ones, so Colombia is the thinner half. Ties fall into four kinds:
- **The song page or a genre page names the song a pasillo.** El alma en los labios, Guayaquil de mis amores, Sombras, Adoración, Mis flores negras, Romance de mi destino, El aguacate, Invernal, Espumas, Oropel, Pueblito viejo, Pescador lucero y río.
- **A performer or composer page names the song a pasillo.** Sendas distintas (Carlota Jaramillo), Tú y yo (Miño Naranjo), En las lejanías (Olimpo Cárdenas), Lamparilla and Esta pena mía (Juan Fernando Velasco), Horas de pasión, Nadie me espera and Mi destino es amarte (Paredes Herrera's pasillo list), La gata golosa and Coqueteos (Fulgencio García), Genio alegre, Blanquita and El trébol agorero (Luis A. Calvo's pasillo list).
- **The track title names the genre.** Carlota Jaramillo's "A Unos Ojos (Pasillo Ecuador)" and "Profunda Herida (Pasillo Ecuador)".
- **Tie by album title only.** Julio Jaramillo's "Náufrago de amor" (Mis mejores pasillos) and Olimpo Cárdenas's "Hacia el calvario" (Los pasillos de oro). Ángel de luz is on Pasillo eterno and is also named a pasillo by English Wikipedia.

**Weak ties and gaps:**
- English Wikipedia is the only source calling "Pescador, lucero y río" and "Pueblito viejo" pasillos. Some Colombian sources may call them bambucos, so these two are the weakest ties.
- Most release years come from store compilations and reissues, not first recordings. The Dúo Ecuador takes are undated (null), and Olimpo Cárdenas's tracks carry 1996 on a ℗ 2006 album.
- Colombian ties rest on Spanish Wikipedia composer pages, which are short. The Venezuelan vals and the Panamanian and Central American pasillo are absent.

The definition rests on the Spanish pages Pasillo (música) and Pasillo ecuatoriano and on English Pasillo. The tree's 1830 fits "las primeras décadas del siglo XIX" only roughly. The size is a judgement from composer catalogue sizes and the 1911 recording count, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Ecuadorian bolero / rockola.** This is Julio Jaramillo's "Nuestro juramento" (1957): a Puerto Rican song he recast as a bolero (Spanish Wikipedia, Nuestro juramento). It is the natural near-miss, but the tree has only `bolero` (Cuban trova and bolero, ended 1960) and `mexbolero` (Mexican), and neither fits an Ecuadorian bolero. The pasillo ecuatoriano page also names Julio Jaramillo as the creator of the "pasillo rockolero" and of música rockolera, which has no id.
- **Sanjuanito, albazo and yaraví.** These are the Ecuadorian forms sung next to the pasillo by the same duets (Benítez-Valencia, Miño Naranjo). Spanish Wikipedia's Sanjuanito and Albazo pages describe them as Ecuadorian genres. `andean` (huayno, panpipes) would be a stretch, so they were not used.
- **Venezuelan vals.** English Wikipedia says Venezuelans call the pasillo "vals". It has no separate id, and none was looked for.

## (d) What fell short
- No Venezuelan, Panamanian or Central American pasillo recording was looked up. The genre's wider reach is in the definition only.
- No MusicBrainz release was used, so no recording dates are confirmed from a second catalogue. Early 78s (the Dúo Ecuador in 1930, Carlota Jaramillo in the 1930s to 1950s, Estudiantina Murillo in 1910 and 1917) are represented only by reissues.
- The Colombian vocal pasillo leans on two duets and English Wikipedia's short song list. Dueto de Antaño has no Spanish Wikipedia page (404), so their many albums were not used. Spanish pages for Espumas, La gata golosa, Pueblito viejo and Enrique Ibáñez Mora do not exist (404).
- "Qué más quieres de mí" (Silva y Villalba, 1976) and Carlos Vieco's own pasillos have no recording found.
- The two most famous pasillos are each in the list once: Guayaquil de mis amores (Dúo Ecuador) and El alma en los labios (Julio Jaramillo). Other versions were left out on purpose.
