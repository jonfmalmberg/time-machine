# tamangselo: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`tamangselo`.** I am fairly sure of the definition and of the songs existing, less sure that each song is a typical Tamang selo, and unsure of the size. All 69 claims are `found`. The definition rests on English Wikipedia (Tamang Selo, Tamang people, Damphu drum, Music of Nepal, Music of Sikkim, Hira Devi Waiba) and a Nepal Traveller feature (damphu drives the rhythm; Lhosar, weddings, harvest; hill districts around the Kathmandu Valley). No page dates the start: the tree's 1800 is kept, and the window names the first recordings found (Radio Nepal 1966, HMV 1974 and 1978). All 26 songs were read from the raw iTunes lookup JSON. The ties are:
- **The title names the genre (title-only ties).** 12 songs: Kutumba, Sur Sudha, Gajab Bahadur Lama Gurung, Raktim Pariwar, the Lokalaya ensemble and Namlo (each a track called "Tamang Selo"/"Tamangselo"), and Tuluk Bahadur Lama, Bukun Gandharba & Amrit Rana, Sac, Deep Chandra Lama, Shailap Waiba, Nirmal Waiba and Jitu Lopchan ("(Tamang Selo)" in the title), plus Mings Lama's "Damphu Ma Selo".
- **The album title names the genre (title-only ties).** 8 songs: the Indreni cassette albums *Ja Changaba - Tamang Selo* (2), *Gangjola Dini - Tamang Selo* and *Tamang Selo Juhari - Helambu Sero*, and the Genius Digital compilation *Tamang Selo* (Mings Lama, Hawal Lama, Kopila Ghising).
- **A page ties the song or performer.** Kunti Moktan's "Mathi Mathi Sailungema" (Kathmandu Post: "played on the beat of Tamang Selo"); Navneet Aditya Waiba's "Jhilke Naachayko" (labelled "Damphu Geet" on her mother's Wikipedia page); Indira Gole Gurung (Tamang Samaj directory: Nepal Selo singer) on *Fapare Selo*.
- **Performer page only (weakest).** Hira Devi Waiba's "Ramri Ta Ramri Kati Sar Ramri" and Navneet Aditya Waiba's "Aye Syangbo": Wikipedia gives Tamang Selo as the singer's genre, but nothing ties these particular songs to it.

**Weak ties and gaps:**
- At least five of the songs are instrumentals (Kutumba, Sur Sudha, Lokalaya, Bukun Gandharba, Deep Chandra Lama). They name the genre's tune but carry no singing.
- Hira Devi Waiba's iTunes date (1917) is a placeholder, so her year is null.
- Kunti Moktan's song is 1998 on iTunes but "early '90s" in the Post.
- The Fapare Selo single names only "selo", not "Tamang".
- Titles are in the Latin spelling the pages use. No readable page gave the Devanagari forms, so none were made up.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Hwai**: the Tamang genealogists' (Tamba) ritual songs. Tamang people on Wikipedia names them; they have no id.
- **Selo nach / Tamang selo dance**: the dance done to these songs. It is not a music genre, so no id is needed.
- **Tamang pop / "pop-selo"**: modern Nepali pop on a selo beat (Kunti Moktan; an iTunes title "Tamang Pop-Selo Song"). It has no id; I kept it inside `tamangselo` rather than stretch `nepalisong`.
- **Other Nepali ethnic folk forms** that sit next to it on folk-tune albums: sebru/shebru, kauda, maruni, sorathi, deuda, jhyaure. None has an id. Dohori (`dohori`) and adhunik geet (`nepalisong`) do have ids and are used as nears.

## (d) What fell short
- The first recorded Tamang selo, Hira Devi Waiba's "Chura ta Hoina Astura", is missing. No recording is on iTunes or MusicBrainz, and the Tamang Online article and the Wayback Machine could not be reached.
- No liner notes, archive catalogue or scholarly source on the genre's history was read. The Ekantipur book article's body loads only by script, and the ACCU (UNESCO) page cited by Wikipedia is only an archived copy, which was unreachable.
- No year is given anywhere for a song being written. Release years come from iTunes and some are reissue or track dates.
- The size is a judgement. The iTunes search shows a very large 2023-2026 singles output, much of it from channel uploaders, which I could not sort.
- I did not do the core singers of the cassette era (for example Ram Thapa or Gopal Yonjan's compositions). No readable page tied a named recording of theirs to the genre.
