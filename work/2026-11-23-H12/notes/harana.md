# harana: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`harana`.** Fairly sure of the definition and the songs, unsure of the size and of the Ruben Tagalog years. All 60 claims are `found`. Track lists come from the raw iTunes lookup JSON. The definition rests on English Wikipedia (Harana (serenade), Kundiman) and on an Asian Journal feature (2020) that quotes guitarist Florante Aguilar on what marks a true harana: the 2/4 habanera rhythm, the solo guitar introduction and interlude, and the archaic lyrics. Each song is tied to harana in two ways at once: the album title names the genre (Introducing the Harana Kings, Harana, The Art of Harana, My Harana), and a page calls the performer a harana singer (Hollywood Bowl for the Harana Kings and Aguilar, Wikipedia for Ruben Tagalog). Only Charmaine Clamor's "Harana sa Dilim" is tied by album and track title alone (her Wikipedia page only names the album). Weak ties:
- Ruben Tagalog's 10 tracks come from a Villar Records collection with ℗ 2006; the original recording dates are on no page read, so their years are null.
- Florante Aguilar's three tracks are solo-guitar instrumentals: true to the harana guitar but without the voice.
- Some Harana Kings and Ruben Tagalog songs (e.g. "Maruja", "Paraluman", "Dalagang Pilipina") may strictly be danzas or kundiman sung at a harana. No page sorts them; they sit on harana-titled albums by harana singers.
- The start date (tree: 1850) is supported only by "believed to have started in the 1800s".

The size is a judgement, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Danza (Philippine dansa)**: the 2/4 habanera-derived song form most harana songs are in. It has no id; `habanera` is the Cuban parent, so I did not use it as a near-miss.
- **Balitaw**: the Visayan and Tagalog sung debate of courtship in triple time. Ruben Tagalog revived balitaws (Wikipedia).
- **Rondalla**: the Filipino plucked-string band (bandurria, octavina) that also plays harana pieces.
- **Visayan (Cebuano) harana**: Stax Huguete, "Usahay", "Matud Nila". Part of this genre, but thinly represented here.
- **Modern "harana" pop**: the Harana boy band of 2015 and many singles titled "Harana". That is `opm`, not this genre.

## (d) What fell short
- No original recording years for Ruben Tagalog (1940s-50s radio and records). A Philippine discography or library record would fix this.
- No recordings of Stax Huguete or Felipe Alonzo (Villar's Ilocano haranas) were found on a readable page.
- No women singers, and nothing from before about 1950 with a date.
- The MusicBrainz release search for "harana" returned nothing; it was not retried.
- The Inquirer pages on the Harana Kings returned only a short shell to the script and were not used.
- The documentary's soundtrack was not identified.
