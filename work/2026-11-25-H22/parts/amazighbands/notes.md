# amazighbands: Amazigh band music (tazenzart and tagroupit)

## Scope decided
The genre is the Moroccan Amazigh **band** movement that started in the Sous in the 1970s, sung in Tashelhit. Its sources name it two ways: **tazenzart** ("the style of Izenzaren", El Guabli 2024; French Wikipedia) and **tagroupit / tiroubba** (the banjo-and-electric-guitar wedding bands; French Wikipedia on Oudaden, and Ben Jones 2024, who calls tagroupit a Tamazight-language sub-genre of the 1970s "bands phenomenon"). Included: Ousmane/Usman (Rabat, 1974-78, Tashelhit poems, so it belongs here although it was not from the Sous), Izenzaren (both bands after the 1975 split), Archach, Oudaden, Ali Chouhad, Ammouri M'barek's solo work after Ousmane, the later banjo bands (Imazzalen, Hassan Wargui's Imanaren and Tiddukla) and the Agadir fusion bands of the 2000s onward (Amarg Fusion, Ribab Fusion, Saghru Band). They are the same movement carried forward, and the brief named them.
Left out: **Khalid Izri and the Rif**. Izri is a solo singer-songwriter in Tarifit. Jones mentions Rif bands such as Tawattun only as a "similar experiment" alongside the Sous bands, and the Rif tradition (izran, Ithran, Walid Mimoun) is a separate language and lineage. Middle Atlas singers (Mohamed Rouicha) are also out: they are solo lotar singers, not bands. Both are listed under (d).

## (a) Confidence
Medium-high for existence, name, dates and the two founding bands: two peer-reviewed articles (Jones 2024; El Guabli 2024) and a 2025 survey (Dominguez) agree, and Wikipedia in three languages and Yabiladi match them. Medium for the parentage. The sources state the tie to the Ghiwane wave outright, but how much weight rways gets against ghiwane is a judgement. Low for the size and the peak window, which are judgements. Song ties: Ousmane, Izenzaren Chamkh and Imanaren songs are tied to the genre by a source naming the song or band. The Izenzaren, Archach, Oudaden, Ali Chouhad, Saghru Band and Hassan Wargui songs are tied **by artist only**: a catalogue page (MusicBrainz or iTunes) shows the song exists, and a separate source puts the band in the genre. Ribab Fusion's tie is the weakest (French Wikipedia: a former Amarg Fusion member's fusion band built on the rways ribab). No tie rests on a title alone. Every one of the 87 claims was found by the checker.

## (c) Disagreements with our records
- `ghiwane` has `ended: 1990`. Jones treats the Amazigh bands as part of the same "bands phenomenon" (Dhahira), and tazenzart and tagroupit are still active. This is not strictly a contradiction (the Darija Ghiwane wave may well have ended), but if amazighbands is its child, the lead may want ghiwane's description to mention the Amazigh offshoot.
- `ghiwane` presence includes DZ and TN peaks. Nothing read here bears on that.
- H14's REPORT says modern bands "drew on rrways poems". Jones confirms this for Ousmane. El Guabli and Dominguez stress the bands' break from the ribab and naqus, so rways is proposed as an `other` parent, not `principal`.
- Within the sources: Ousmane's founding is 1974 (Yabiladi, active 1974-78) or 1975 (El Guabli). Who recorded first in Tamazight is also disputed: Jones says Ousmane, French Wikipedia says Izenzaren's first cassette came in 1974. Izenzaren formed in 1972 (English Wikipedia), 1972-73 (El Guabli) or the late 1960s (French Wikipedia).

## (d) Neighbours with no id
- **Rif Amazigh song** (Tarifit: Khalid Izri, Walid Mimoun, bands such as Tawattun and Thidrin). It deserves its own id if the lead wants Rif coverage.
- **Middle Atlas Amazigh song** (Mohamed Rouicha, Hammou Oulyazid; lotar and bendir, Tamazight). It sits between `amazighahwashandah` and modern song.
- **Amazigh rock / desert-rock of south-east Morocco** (Meteor Airlines, Jubantouja, Tasuta N-Imal). It is a newer stream (The Continent 2025; Songlines). It could be a sub-genre here or a later id; I kept only Saghru Band.
- `desertblues` covers Tuareg guitar and is not a neighbour in sound.

## (e) What fell short
- No song by **Amarg Fusion**: none of its albums (Agadir Ifawne 2006, Argan d'Oufgan) has a page a script could read. Ribab Fusion stands in for the fusion branch.
- Ousmane has no catalogue page (MusicBrainz and iTunes found nothing). Its four songs rest on Jones's article alone, with 1976 from Africangrooves.
- Nine songs have no year (from Wikipedia lists or the articles). The iTunes dates of the Sous labels' digitised cassettes are reissue dates and were not used as release years, except where the ℗ line gives the original year (1976, 1981, 1982).
- No source gives a peak; 1975-1995 is my judgement.
- Wikipedia's API refused scripts at first (HTTP 429); plain page fetches worked.
- 87 claims, more than the target of about 60. They were needed because most songs need one claim for existence and another for the genre tie.
