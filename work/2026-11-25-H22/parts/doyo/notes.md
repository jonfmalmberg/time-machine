# Dōyō (`doyo`): researcher's notes, H22, read 2026-11-25

## (a) Confidence
High on what dōyō is, when it began and that it is separate from shōka: Japanese Wikipedia (童謡, 赤い鳥, かなりや, song and composer articles) and the English stub agree on 1918, Akai Tori, the first song set to music (Kanariya, 1919), the poets and composers, the 1920s golden age and the boom after 1945. Medium on the playlist: every song is shown on a dated store listing (iTunes Japan lookup, with the artist, the album and the track checked as one string), and every album but one names 童謡 in its title. The weak ties: Honda Nobuko's three Noguchi/Motoori songs and Hirai Hideko's 十五夜お月さん sit on an album named for the lyricist (七つの子～野口雨情作品集), not for the genre; they are tied by Hirai being called a dōyō singer and by the song pages calling 七つの子, 赤い靴 and 青い眼の人形 dōyō. Ties by album title only: 平井英子 てるてる坊主 (中山晋平の童謡; Hirai is also called a dōyō singer) and 杉田和子ほか 背くらべ (also called a dōyō on its song page). 芹洋子 かなりや and 中村邦子 月の沙漠 are tied by the album title (心のふるさと 懐かしの童謡 ベスト, 童謡誕生ストーリー第1話) and the song pages. Release years 1928-1932 rest on an inference (see unverified.json). The claim count (83) is over the brief's 60: years and ties for each song took two claims, and I judged the song-level ties worth it.

## (c) Disagreements with our records
- None with `data/`. `shokaandgunka`'s record does not mention dōyō; the sources treat dōyō as its replacement and a separate concept (ja 故郷 (唱歌): 「唱歌」と「童謡」は、厳密には別個の概念である), which supports a separate genre rather than a sub-genre.
- Between the sources: ja 童謡 says Akai Tori and Kin no Hoshi both closed in 1929; ja 赤い鳥 says Akai Tori was suspended from February 1929 to January 1931 and ran until 1936. I used neither date.

## (d) Neighbours with no id
- Warabe-uta (わらべうた): traditional children's game and counting songs, which dōyō is defined against. Not `minyo`.
- Shin-min'yō (新民謡): composed "new folk songs" of the same Noguchi/Nakayama team from the 1920s; used `minyo` as the nearest id for the near.
- Dōkyoku (童曲): children's songs for koto by Miyagi Michio, before Kanariya (ja 童謡 article).
- "Atarashii kodomo no uta" of the Roba no Kai and NHK Minna no Uta (from the late 1950s): the children's song that followed dōyō.
- Wartime shōkokumin-ka (少国民歌) children's songs.
- Korean dongyo (동요): the Korean counterpart; `changga` is the nearest id.

## (e) What fell short
- No early record by Motoori Midori (named as "Japan's first dōyō singer") or of the 1920 Kanariya disc could be found on a readable store or catalogue.
- MusicBrainz had nothing useful for 平井英子 or 本居みどり (a name search returned a modern band), so all recordings come from iTunes.
- The playlist leans on Victor (Hirai Hideko) and Columbia (Kawada Masako) reissues; King and Nipponophone's prewar 78s are missing, and so are the Akai Tori composer Narita Tamezō's own recordings.
- Ja Wikipedia's article API was rate-limited (HTTP 429); the plain article pages were read instead.
- Dropped to keep the count down: 春よ来い, 夕焼小焼, シャボン玉, こがねむし, 蛙の笛 (all recordings were found; the cut was mine).

## (f) Questions for the lead
1. Parent: `shokaandgunka` as principal is a judgement (the sources set dōyō against shōka, they do not say it descends from it). Should there be an `other` parent from `minyo`, given Noguchi Ujō's folk-song background and the yonanuki scale? No source in hand says so.
2. Children: Hirai Hideko and Fujiyama Ichirō began as dōyō singers and moved to popular song, and record companies filed anime songs as dōyō for tax (ja 童謡). Should `doyo` be an `other` parent of `kayokyoku` or `anison`? I did not propose either.
3. `ended`: null, though ja Wikipedia says the narrow literary dōyō ended around 1973. Keep null?
