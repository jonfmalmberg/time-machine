# copla: Copla (canción española)

## (a) Confidence
High that the genre is real, distinct and correctly described. Spanish and English Wikipedia agree on what copla is and who sang and wrote it. They also agree on when it flourished (1930s–1940s, then 1950s film) and when it declined (1960s). Each of the 24 songs has two found claims. One is a track row on an iTunes Spain compilation whose title names the copla. The other is a row in the Spanish Wikipedia list of classic coplas, or a line on the singer's page. That ties each song to the genre by a source, not by title alone. The genre tie on the iTunes side is through the compilation title ("Antología de la Copla", "Las 101 grandes Coplas"). These are the weak points:
- Neither compilation says whether a track is the original 78 or a later re-recording, so every release year is null.
- The begin date (1917, El Relicario) is "considered" the first copla, not a documented first use of the name.
- The peak window and the Seville origin are judgements.

## (c) Disagreements with our records
- **`iberianpop`** is named "Iberian popular song and guitar (romance, copla, décima)" and covers 1500–1800. In that name, "copla" is the old stanza form. English Wikipedia says the 20th-century song genre is "deriving from the poetic form of the same name" (copla:14). The two are related in name and verse, not in sound. The modern copla should not be a child of `iberianpop` and should not be folded into it. Suggest the record's name say "copla (verse)" or drop the word, so a listener looking for Concha Piquer does not land in 1500–1800.
- **Proposed parents.** `zarzuela` as principal, `flamenco` and `pasodoble` as other. These are judgements; see parentage.json.
- **`pasodoble`.** Its record and playlist already hold sung pasodobles by copla singers: El relicario, Mi jaca, Francisco Alegre, Campanera, Suspiros de España, En tierra extraña. The Spanish Wikipedia copla list counts all of these as coplas, and says El Relicario (1917) is the first copla. Nothing in pasodoble is wrong. Its "pasodoble canción" half is a sub-form of copla, so the two overlap. I kept those songs out of the copla playlist to avoid duplicates. Copla's `began` of 1917 rests on a song that is linked to pasodoble.
- **Existing song.** The data already holds "La niña de fuego" (Lola Flores and Manolo Caracol, 1944). The copla list credits the song to Manolo Caracol in 1944. My playlist uses Manolo Caracol's solo track. The master-table step should decide whether these are one song or two recordings.

## (d) Neighbours with no id
- **Cuplé.** Spain, 1890s–1930s: risqué variety-theatre song (Raquel Meller, La Fornarina). Both Wikipedias call it the direct source of copla, so it would be copla's principal parent if added.
- **Tonadilla escénica.** 18th century: the short sung theatre piece behind cuplé.
- **Ópera flamenca.** 1920s–1950s: flamenco in theatres and bullrings (Pepe Marchena), an antecedent of copla. Now partly under `flamenco`.
- **Revista musical.** Madrid revue (Celia Gámez, Las Leandras, 1931), copla's stage neighbour.
- **Nueva copla.** 1990s onward: Pasión Vega, Diana Navarro. A later sub-genre; could be listed as a sub-genre of copla.

## (e) What fell short
- No release years. The tracks come from re-release compilations, and none of them says it uses the original recordings. A MusicBrainz or national-library (BNE) discography pass could date the original 78s and EPs. I did not try, to stay within the MusicBrainz budget.
- No source states a peak; 1933–1960 is a judgement.
- Later presence is thin. Mexico rests on one fact. Argentina and Cuba were seen but left out.
- The genre's early end (1917–1932: Raquel Meller, Pastora Imperio, Angelillo's first records) is under-represented. The playlist starts in 1933.
- At 84 claims I went over the budget of about 60. Each song carries two claims (existence and genre tie).

## (f) Questions for the lead
1. Should cuplé be added as its own genre? It would become copla's principal parent, and zarzuela would become "other".
2. Should `iberianpop`'s name be changed so "copla" no longer points at 1500–1800?
3. Should El relicario, Mi jaca, Francisco Alegre and Campanera also be linked to copla? The copla sources claim them; the pasodoble playlist holds them now. Linking them to both genres would give copla its own first song (1917).
4. Tradition: I used `iberian`, the same as pasodoble. `flamencot` is the alternative if the tree groups by Andalusian sound rather than by Spanish popular song.
