# maloya: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`maloya`.** I am fairly sure of the songs and the definition, and much less sure of the size. All 80 claims are `found`. Every track and album was read from the raw MusicBrainz web service JSON. Each one is tied to maloya in one of three ways:
- **The title names the genre.** This covers 13 songs: Firmin Viry's Ocora album *Île de la Réunion: Maloya*, his 1976 PCR record *Le Maloya et le IVe Congrès*, the 1976 compilation *Peuple du Maloya*, *Bourbon Maloya*, Lo Rwa Kaf's *Tradition Maloya*, Gramoun Bébé's *Le maloya kabaré*, and the tracks "Maloya zordi", "Destin maloya", "Maloya Power", "Oyé Maloya" and Alain Péters' "Wayo maman (maloya)".
- **A performer page calls them maloya musicians.** This covers 12 songs. English Wikipedia credits Firmin Viry with rescuing maloya and names Ziskakan. French Wikipedia says Granmoun Lélé left over 200 maloya titles, calls Waro a "poète du maloya", places Lindigo in the maloya tradition and says Zanmari Baré plays kayamb and bobre.
- **Salem Tradition** is tied only through Christine Salem. French Wikipedia lists her among maloya musicians and says she founded the group.

The definition rests on English and French Wikipedia: the instruments, call and response, the ternary pulse, sacred and secular forms, the ban until 1981, the first public performance in 1959, and electric maloya from the late 1970s. The start date is "période d'esclavage … dès le XVIIIe siècle", which fits the tree's 1750 only roughly; no source gives a year.

**Weak ties and gaps:**
- Ziskakan's "Bato fou" (1983) is the band-pop end of the genre.
- Alain Péters' track stands in for electric maloya.
- Two years conflict between sources: Soleye is 1991 on MusicBrainz and 1995 on French Wikipedia; Foutan Fonnker is 2000 on MusicBrainz and 1999 on French Wikipedia.
- The two Bourbon Maloya tracks have no year.
- Gramoun Sello is missing: no article body and no release was found.

The size is a judgement from group lists and catalogue sizes, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Electric maloya (maloya électrique), 1975–1985.** Examples: Caméléon, Michou, the Oté Maloya compilation of 2017 (MusicBrainz 87c890e0-0b39-468c-8c62-1768cef2a759).
- **Electronic maloya.** Examples: Jako Maron's *The Electro Maloya Experiments*, the *Digital Kabar* compilation, Labelle's *Post-Maloya*.
- **Maloggae (maloya with reggae).** Examples: Baster, Naéssayé, Progression.
- **Maloyaz (maloya with jazz).** Examples: Meddy Gerville, Sabouk.
- **Servis kabaré / malgas ritual music.** This is the sacred ancestor-rite form, which French Wikipedia separates from stage maloya.

Séga of Réunion and of Mauritius both fall under the existing `sega` id, so the two near-misses go there.

## (d) What fell short
- **Gramoun Sello:** the French Wikipedia URL returned a page with no article body, and a MusicBrainz artist search found 0 releases. Nothing was added. Redo this with another spelling (for example "Granmoun Sello") or with Discogs.
- **Alain Péters' *Rest' la Maloya* (2016):** the MusicBrainz release 9aff40df-528e-4332-831f-e48aecb9dc71 returned "server busy" and was not retried. The title song is not in the playlist.
- **Year conflicts:** Soleye (1991 on MusicBrainz, 1995 on French Wikipedia) and Foutan Fonnker (2000 on MusicBrainz, 1999 on French Wikipedia). The playlist uses the MusicBrainz year.
- **Bourbon Maloya:** the release has no date on its MusicBrainz record.
- **Firmin Viry's 1972 single** is known only from French Wikipedia's discography.
- **Nothing before 1976** has a tracklist, so the playlist's oldest songs are the PCR records of 1976.
- **Not researched:** Baster, Ti Fock, Granmoun Baba solo albums, Françoise Guimbert, Simangavole and Nathalie Natiembé. Researching them would deepen the 1980s–2000s.
- **The size** is not based on any catalogue count.
