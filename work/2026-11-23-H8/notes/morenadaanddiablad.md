# morenadaanddiablad: notes

## (a) Confidence

Confidence is medium in the definition, medium in the songs and low in the size. The definition comes from English and Spanish Wikipedia (Morenada, Diablada, Carnival of Oruro / Carnaval de Oruro):
- instruments: matraca, cymbal, bass drum, trumpet, tuba;
- "ritmo lento y paso cansino";
- the diablada's March and Devil's Mecapaqueña;
- siku bands replaced by brass bands;
- Puno and La Tirana (Chile);
- dates: Thola's estimate of c. 1790, morenada scores from 1860 at Poopó, a diablada score from 1862, the first organised diablada in 1904, Puno brass diablada from 1962.

The tree's start of 1800 fits. All 54 claims were read raw from Wikipedia, the iTunes lookup API or the MusicBrainz web service, and the checker finds every one.

Sixteen of the 25 songs are brass-band recordings:
- Banda Espectacular Pagador: three albums whose track titles all say "Morenada", plus a 1964 "Popurrí de Morenadas" by Banda "Sebastian Pagador";
- the Puno bands Super Impacto and Banda Policía Nacional del Perú: titles all say "Diablada";
- Banda Intercontinental Poopó: one diablada medley;
- the Brass Unbound "Diana / Morenada";
- an Oruro field recording.

The other nine are folk-group pieces that MusicBrainz names as morenada or diablada by title (Inti-Illimani, Illapu ×2, Ariel Ramírez & Jaime Torres, Jaime Torres, Zulma Yugar, Enriqueta Ulloa, Tupay, Kjarkas).

Weak ties:
- In every case the genre tie comes from the track or album title, not from a prose page about the band. No Wikipedia article exists for Banda Pagador.
- The Brass Unbound credit "Banda Espectular Parador de Oruro / Banda Poopò" is my reading of a misspelling.
- Several years are compilation dates: Kjarkas 35 años (2006), the Azoguini anthology (2019) and the PNP album (2004; one track title says 2005).
- The Ramírez/Torres and Jaime Torres "La diablada" are charango and piano versions that are tied only by their titles.
- Northern Chile is not represented in the playlist: its bands appear on iTunes only as singles, which I did not read.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- Caporales (with saya caporal): the dance closest to the morenada at Oruro and Puno. Tupay's "Soy Caporal (Saya)" (1998) and the Kjarkas' "Saya Cochabamba" are on the same pages.
- Saya afroboliviana.
- Tinku, and the pre-brass sikuri / sicumoreno ensembles that once accompanied the Puno diablada.
- Diablada pillareña (Ecuador).
- Northern Chilean "bailes religiosos" bands of La Tirana.
- The Uruguayan candombe comparsa named "Morenada" is a different thing that shares the name. `candombe` exists in the tree, but no page read ties that comparsa to candombe, so it is not used as a near.

## (d) Gaps, to redo

- The Poopó bands' 1991 Lauro Records albums and the 2006 Intercontinental Poopó album do not label their tracks, so they are unused.
- Northern Chile (Arica, Tocopilla, Antofagasta, Iquique) diablada bands: only singles were seen in the iTunes search; their track lists were not read.
- Los Calchakis, *Au pays de la diablada* (1979): no track is labelled.
- Pagador track titles carry years (1945–2002) that look like composition years. A sourced composer and year list for the classic morenadas (e.g. "Mantilla de vicuña", "Rosas de Viacha") would make the window and the core list stronger.
- No pre-1964 recording was found.
