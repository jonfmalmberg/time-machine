# Lavani (`lavani`): research notes, 2026-11-23

## (a) Confidence

Medium-to-good confidence in the songs, medium in the definition, low in the size. Every one of the 25 songs has a `found` claim on a page that ties it to lavani in its own words: 24 on the song pages of Aathavanitli Gani, a non-commercial Marathi song-lyrics archive whose "गीत प्रकार" (song type) field reads लावणी for each, and "Chandra" on Wikipedia, whose infobox gives "Genre Filmi Lavani". Fourteen songs also have a second tie on Wikipedia or the Times of India, e.g. Wikipedia's Sulochana Chavan page ("known for her lavanis", naming "Kasa Kay Patil Bara Hay Ka?", "Solaawa Varees Dhokyacha", "Padarawarti Jartaricha"), Sangtye Aika ("The lavani song \"Bugadi Majhi Sandli Ga\""), the song pages for Apsara Aali, Wajle Ki Bara and Ugavali Shukrachi Chandani ("Genre ... lavani"), and the Times of India's 2018 list ("Chabidar Chabi ... an evergreen Lavani song"). The main weak tie is that 11 songs depend on the one archive alone (its genre tag is an editor's judgement, and the archive is fan-run, though curated). The playlist is lopsided toward film lavani. The definition rests on English Wikipedia's Lavani page, which has several "citation needed" tags; the facts used (dholki, quick tempo, phadachi/baithakichi, Honaji Bala and the tabla, 1560s / Peshwa-era rise, poet-singer dates) are in its sourced paragraphs, but the page is thin. The years come from Wikipedia film release dates, or from the archive's film pages where Wikipedia has nothing. Where the archive disagrees with Wikipedia (four films), Wikipedia is used. Four songs have no year. Size is a judgement, not a count.

**Tree record question (not changed):** the tree gives `tradition: punjabi` for lavani. Lavani is Marathi (Maharashtra), with no Punjabi link, so this looks like a placeholder. A Marathi/Maharashtrian folk tradition id, or one shared with other western-Indian folk forms, would fit better. The same label sits on `garbaanddandiyaraa` (Gujarati) and `bihusongs` (Assamese), so it is probably a catch-all for "Indian regional folk" and worth a look by the lead. Origin Pune and began 1700 fit the sources (Peshwa-era Pune; Wikipedia gives beginnings in the 1560s and rise under the late Peshwas).

## (c) Natural neighbours with no id in the tree

- Tamasha (the Marathi folk-theatre form that lavani lives inside; sangeet bari and dholki bhari troupes).
- Powada (Marathi heroic ballad of the shahirs).
- Gavlan / gaulan (Krishna-and-milkmaid songs of the tamasha opening; Natarang's soundtrack pairs "Lavani and Gavalan").
- Gondhal and bharud (Marathi devotional/folk performance songs).
- Marathi bhavgeet and Marathi film song generally (the tree has `filmi` for Hindi film song only). Many of these lavanis are also Marathi film songs.
- Natya sangeet (Marathi stage music).
- Rajasthani and Kannada devotional songs also called "lavani" (e.g. Tejaji ki lavani, Karibasaveshwara Lavani, seen on iTunes) are a different, unrelated use of the word and must not be folded in.

## (d) Shortfalls (to redo)

- No recording of a working tamasha troupe or of Yamunabai Waikar was found on a script-readable page (iTunes search for her name returned nothing; MusicBrainz search for "lavani" returned no relevant releases). Field and troupe recordings (sangeet bari) are absent, so the list is the film and gramophone layer.
- No Hindi-film lavani or Hindi item song called a lavani by a page was found. The two near-misses (`filmi`) are tied by what the pages do say (Chikni Chameli "is an item song" by the Natarang composers; Dhagala Lagali Kala's genre is "Folk, filmi, pop, dance"), not by a page comparing them with lavani.
- Baithakichi lavani is thin: only Shobha Gurtu (two songs) and nothing explicitly labelled baithakichi. A search of iTunes for "baithakichi lavani" returned nothing.
- The cassette-era stage stars (Surekha Punekar, Roshan Satarkar, Sandhya Mane, Mangala Bansode) have one song (Roshan Satarkar) and two albums. Their iTunes catalogue exists (Surekha Punekar's compilation, Roshan Satarkar's albums), but the track listings were not checked against a lavani tie.
- iTunes album dates (1972, 1985, 1994 for Sulochana Chavan) are digital-store dates and may not be original release years. The album list does not give years.
- Marathi Wikipedia (mr.wikipedia.org) was not read; it may list more lavanis and recordings.
