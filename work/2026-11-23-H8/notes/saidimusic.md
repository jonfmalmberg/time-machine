# saidimusic: Sa'idi music

## (a) Confidence

Medium confidence in the definition and the core of the playlist. Size and the start date are low. The definition rests on four sources. English Wikipedia's Music of Egypt says Sa'idi is folk music from Upper Egypt and that the Musicians of the Nile are its best-known group. The Real World Records pages for Luxor to Isna and Charcoal Gypsies describe the two-string rababa, the "mizmar sa'idi" with circular breathing, and the Mataqil clan, whose musicians "go back seven generations". The Naïade Productions press kit for Les Musiciens du Nil, in French and English, says the mizmar plays with the tabla baladî for the tahtib stick dance, "danse traditionnelle du Sa'id", and the band plays at weddings and mawlids. Wikipedia's Tahtib page says the dance's music is tabl and mizmar. Every one of the 78 claims was found by the checker.

The playlist has 25 songs. Eleven are by the Musicians of the Nile, tied by performer pages. Six are by Metqal Qenawi and Shamandi Metqal, also tied by performer pages. The rest are tied by album titles that name the genre.

The weak ties:
- **Metqal Qenawi's El Wady Music singles.** Their only date is the label's ℗ 1985 line on a digital reissue, so it may not be the recording year.
- **Shamandy Metqal.** He is matched to the Shamandi Tewfiq Metqal on the Real World page by name only.
- **Belly-dance studio albums.** The Upper Egypt Ensemble and Aswan Music & Dance Ensemble albums (Hollywood Music Center, 2000 and 2006) were made for that market. They count only because their titles say "Saidi".
- **Late tracks.** Funoon Shaabeya (2023) and El Rayes Mahrous El Sedfawy (℗ 1990) are tied only because the track titles say "mizmar sa'idi".

The tree's start date of 1700 is not supported by any page. The oldest evidence is a family seven generations deep and recordings from 1976. Hilali epic singing is in the list only through the Musicians of the Nile's own "Abou Zeïd el Hilali". The Naïade press kit (quoting Ocora) puts "geste hilalienne" in their Upper Egyptian repertoire. Wikipedia assigns the sira to both Upper and Lower Egypt, so sira singers in general are kept out. The size is a judgement; no source counts recordings.

## (c) Natural neighbours with no id in the tree
- Sirat Bani Hilal epic singing (Upper and Lower Egypt; UNESCO-listed), as a tradition of its own.
- Egyptian mizmar baladi / fellahin (Delta) wedding music.
- Sawahli simsimiyya music of the Suez Canal coast (el-Tanboura).
- Sa'idi-rhythm pop and belly-dance "saidi" pieces from Cairo, which sit between this genre and `shaabi`/`arabpop`.
- `nubianmusic` exists and borders this genre to the south (Aswan), but no dated Nubian recording was read, so it is not used as a near-miss.

## (d) What fell short
- No page dates the start of the genre. The tree's 1700 rests on judgement plus "seven generations" (Real World). A source on 19th-century Upper Egyptian rababa or mizmar players (for example Lane's Modern Egyptians, on Project Gutenberg) was not reached: the search timed out.
- Ahmed Mougahid/Megahid: a "Sheikh Ahmed Megahed" release (℗ 1983) was found on iTunes, but nothing ties it to the Sa'idi singer, so it is left out (see unverified). No recordings were found on a readable page for Sohar Magdy or for Shamandy (the singer).
- The Metqal Qinawi LP (Akenaton, 2017) has no readable track list. Forced Exposure returned 403, and the Discogs search needs a token.
- AllMusic (403) and Smithsonian Folkways ("Music of Upper Egypt") were not read.
- The Metqal Qenawi tracks have only the El Wady ℗ 1985 line as a date. The original recording years are unknown.
- The near-miss "Zahma ya Dunia, Zahma" has no year on any page read. Inta Omri uses Wikipedia's prose date of 1965; its infobox says February 1964.
