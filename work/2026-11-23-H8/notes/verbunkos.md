# verbunkos: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`verbunkos`.** Confidence medium-high for the definition and the playlist, low for the size. Scope: Hungarian recruiting-dance music. First the Romani band tradition: the Hungarian State Folk Ensemble orchestra (Angel LP, 1955), Sándor Lakatos and Lajos Boross with Imre Csenki in the 1950s (Frémeaux reissue), the Hungarian Gypsy Orchestra playing Bihari's *Spur Verbunk* and a verbunk of 1848, Sándor Déki Lakatos, András Farkas Jr., Kálmán Balogh and Méta, and Tcha Limberger's Budapest Gypsy Orchestra. Then early verbunkos on period instruments (Capella Savaria). Then the village verbunk from Szatmár, Rábaköz, Kapuvár, Karcag, Gyimes, the Palóc region, the Kun region and the Székely lands, played by village Romani musicians (Aladár Csiszár, the Szászcsávás Band) and by táncház bands (Sebő, Muzsikás, Hegedős, Ökrös, Csík). The definition rests on English and Hungarian Wikipedia (*Verbunkos*, *János Bihari*, *Csárdás*), which give the lassú and friss pair, the dotted rhythm, the running-note friss, ornamented violin figures, augmented seconds, a band of violins and cimbalom, the 18th-century origin and recruiting up to 1848/49. All 23 songs come from MusicBrainz release JSON, and in every case the track title itself names the genre ("verbunk", "verbunkos", "körverbunk"). That tie is honest but shallow: no liner note was read. Weak points:
- Lakatos and Boross–Csenki are dated only "1954–1959" by the reissue title.
- The Capella Savaria disc has no date on its page.
- The Hungarian Gypsy Orchestra disc (1992) has no label on MusicBrainz.
- The táncház tracks (six of the 23) also fit `tanchaz`. The lead should decide whether a village verbunk played by a revival band belongs here, there or in both.
- Bihari-, Lavotta- and Csermák-era pieces whose titles do not say verbunk were left out. As a result the art-verbunkos repertoire is under-represented compared with the brief's scope.

Near-misses:
- Bartók's *Contrasts*, "Verbunkos" movement (Goodman, Szigeti and Bartók; release dated 1996) → `modernism`. The brief suggested romantic or classical, but modernism exists and fits a 1938 work.
- Haydn's "Gypsy Rondo" (Florestan Trio) → `classical`.
- Liszt's Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2 (Cziffra, Prague 1955) → `romantic`.
- A Déki Lakatos nóta medley → `magyarnota`.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- Csárdás (the Hungarian couple dance and its Gypsy-band music that grew out of verbunkos). It is close to `magyarnota` but not the same thing.
- Slovácko verbuňk (Moravian recruit dances, UNESCO 2008) and Slovak verbunk, which are the Czech and Slovak branches of the dance.
- Legényes (the Transylvanian men's dance of Kalotaszeg, related to the verbunk). Today it would fall under `tanchaz`.
- Palotás (the 19th-century ballroom offshoot).

## (d) What fell short
- No liner note or Hungarian archive page was read. Every song is tied to the genre only by its track title on MusicBrainz. The next step would be Hungaroton catalogue records or Folkeurópa/Fonó booklet PDFs, to bring in titled Bihari, Lavotta and Csermák pieces (see unverified.json).
- No true field recordings were found: Bartók's and Kodály's phonograph cylinders, the *Magyar népzene* anthology and the *Elveszett Éden* archive set were not looked up for verbunk tracks.
- The recording years of the Frémeaux 1950s tracks and of the Capella Savaria disc are unknown.
- The Lujza Tari article PDF (cited on the Csárdás page) returned an HTML error page.
- MusicBrainz was often "busy". All release pages finally loaded, and the checker's cache holds them.
