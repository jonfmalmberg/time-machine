# sevillanas: notes

## (a) Confidence
Medium-high for the definition and the playlist, low for the size. The definition rests on raw English and Spanish Wikipedia (Sevillanas; Feria de Abril; the pages of Los Romeros de la Puebla, Los Marismeños, Manuel Pareja Obregón): seguidilla roots from the late 16th century, a distinct dance from the 18th, the modern form with the Seville Fair in the later 19th, always four sevillanas in triple time (en.wikipedia says it is "generally 6/8", es.wikipedia says 3/4), guitar, palmas and castanets, the rociera kind with flauta and tamboril, choral harmony from the coros rocieros, bass and electric instruments from Los Marismeños, orchestral arrangements from Pareja Obregón. Every song was read from raw page text: thirteen from MusicBrainz release JSON (album or track title naming sevillanas: the Saura soundtrack of 1992, El Pali's Sevillanas 1982, Amigos de Gines and Los Marismeños Sevillanas 1988, Pareja Obregón's Sevillanas antológicas 1983, the Hermanos Toronjo's and Cantores de Híspalis's Sevillanas de oro, María del Monte's Reina de las Sevillanas, Los del Río's Cantemos por sevillanas, Martirio's He visto color por sevillanas, the 1925 Odeon Sevillanas números 3 y 4, the compilation Sevillanas de los Cuarenta), two from iTunes lookup JSON (Las Corraleras de Lebrija's Sevillanas de Lebrija (Sevillanas corraleras); Rocío Jurado's "Rocieras de Rocío A (Sevillanas)"), and the rest from Wikipedia. The checker found all 91 claims. Weak ties: Los del Río are represented by one song from a 2019 reissue (album dated 1990 on en.wikipedia); Los Romeros de la Puebla only by their medley on the Saura soundtrack; Cantores de Híspalis's "A bailar, a bailar" and the Toronjo song come from later compilations, so their years are open; Martirio's He visto color is an ironic, modern take that sits at the edge of the genre; Rocío Jurado's iTunes year (1962) is implausible and not used. The three copla singers come from an undated compilation.

## (c) Natural neighbours with no id in the tree
- Spanish copla / canción española (Concha Piquer, Rocío Jurado, Lola Flores as copla singers): no id; `iberianpop` is the 1500-1800 romance/copla tradition and was not stretched.
- Fandangos de Huelva (sung by the same groups: Los Marismeños, Paco Toronjo, El Pali): no id of its own; could be under `flamenco`.
- Seguidillas (manchegas, alosneras) and the escuela bolera dances (sevillanas boleras): the parent forms, no id.
- Coros rocieros and Rocío salves / Misa rociera: no id.
- Rumba as played by sevillanas groups (Los del Río, Los Marismeños) goes to `rumbaflam`, used for two near-misses.

A later run could read release track lists for Los Romeros de la Puebla's annual albums, Los Hermanos Reyes, Salmarina, Ecos del Rocío, Siempre Así and the coros rocieros.
- The Romeros' iTunes collection 695061840 ("Las Mejores Sevillanas de los Romeros de la Puebla") returned no tracks on lookup; the iTunes search results were not used as evidence because they change over time.
- Los del Río's 1970s albums titled Sevillanas y rumbas (en.wikipedia) had no track list read.
- Release years of the Toronjo, Cantores de Híspalis and Sevillanas de los Cuarenta tracks are unknown (compilations) and left null.
- Niña de los Peines's sevillanas, named first among solo singers on es.wikipedia, were not found on a readable page.
