# rara: notes

## (a) Confidence

Medium confidence in the songs, medium in the definition, low in the size. All 72 claims were found by the checker. Every track was read from a raw page a script can read: the iTunes lookup API (Caribbean Revels, Soul Jazz's Rara in Haiti), the MusicBrainz web service (Rhythms of Rapture, Soul Jazz date and catalogue number, Caribbean Revels date), the University of Wisconsin library record of the Alan Lomax Haiti box (track list of Volume 4, "Rara: vodou in motion", with band names in brackets), and two Folkways liner-note PDFs with a clean text layer (FE 4531, 1978; FE 4432, 1952). Each track is tied to rara or gagá by the album title (Caribbean Revels: Haitian Rara and Dominican Gaga; Rara in Haiti; Rara: vodou in motion), by the band's own name (Bann Rara ..., Rara Sitesolèy, Rara Ya Sezi), or by the liner notes calling it a rara song (Fiole, Balile). The definition rests on English Wikipedia (Rara music, Léogâne), Haitian Creole Wikipedia (Léogâne and its more than 30 bands; gagá in the Dominican Republic), Spanish Wikipedia (gagá instruments) and Verna Gillis's FE 4531 notes (vaksin in hocket, tambour, satirical songs, gagá as Haitian-Dominican rara). Weak ties: (1) years are release years, while the sound is much older (Lomax 1936–37, Gillis 1976–78, McAlister 1993); Lomax tracks carry 2009 and Courlander/Stearns 1952. (2) Soul Jazz's iTunes issue is dated 2026 and credits only "Rara Group"; 2010 comes from MusicBrainz's CD record, and the recordist was not found. (3) Caribbean Revels tracks are named after places and bands, not songs; their artist names come from iTunes ("Gaga band, Dominican Republic", "Davilmar Vilma & Vapeur"). (4) Two Courlander tracks credit an unidentified band recorded by Marshall Stearns; the artist string is ours. (5) Gagá has only the five Caribbean Revels tracks; no Dominican-made gagá release was verified. The size is a judgement, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree

- Rara tech: electronic dance music on rara rhythms (Gardy Girault); English Wikipedia has it as a section of Rara music.
- Gagá as its own Dominican genre. It is folded in here, as the brief asks; the tree has no `gaga` id.
- Haitian kanaval (carnival) bands and their yearly carnival songs (konpa and rasin bands on floats): no id. Mizik rasin (`mizikrasin`) takes the rara-roots bands.
- Vodou ceremonial drumming: no id (the Rhythms of Rapture album is mainly that).

## (d) What fell short

- No Dominican-made gagá record found; Dominican gagá rests only on Gillis's 1976–77 recordings. A CUNY Dominican Studies page on "Sí Gagá" (Roldán) returned a bot check.
- Sounds of the Universe (Soul Jazz shop) returned a bot check, so the recordist and recording year of Soul Jazz's Rara in Haiti are unknown.
- The Caribbean Revels liner PDF (SFW40402) is a bad OCR scan, so nothing was quoted from its text except its heading line. Kònè (zinc horn) is described there but cannot be quoted. The FE 4531 PDF is clean and was used instead.
- Lomax Volume 4 has about 30 items, but the catalogue gives the performer only once after each run of titles. Only the title next to each name was used. Le Roux Chay-o-pye's run of 13 songs is left out (it could be added from the box's own booklet).
- Releases by present-day Léogâne bands (cassettes, YouTube, streaming) were not searched beyond one Bandcamp search, which found nothing usable.
- The Courlander "Vaccines" track (a bamboo trumpet band) is on one of three Music of Haiti LPs, and which one was not checked.
