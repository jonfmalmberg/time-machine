## What was asked
The next eight pending genres, as H7: tree order of `data/genres.json`, a genre being pending when its line has no `definition` and it is in no `work/*/defs.add.json` on `dev` or on a `helper/*` branch (the 24 of H3, H4 and H7 were skipped). The eight: `saidimusic` (Sa'idi music), `lavani` (Lavani), `maloya` (Maloya), `rara` (Rara), `huapango` (Huapango (son huasteco)), `verbunkos` (Verbunkos), `morenadaanddiablad` (Morenada and diablada), `sevillanas` (Sevillanas). For each: a size estimate first, then a definition and a playlist in the shape of the H7 hand-back, following the evidence section of `work/README.md`: quotes copied from raw page text fetched from the shell, and only songs whose claim the check script finds go into the add files. Titles in standard spelling with diacritics, page spellings as `alias`; neighbours with no id named, not stretched.

## What is in this folder
- `defs.add.json`: eight new definition entries, one per line (window, sound, three markers, fits, near).
- `playlists.add.json`: eight new playlist entries, one per line (status, intro, peak, albums, fold, note, yrs).
- `sizes.json`: genre id → low, high, core, basis, confidence.
- `sources.json`: genre id → list of claims (kind def/size/fit/near/song/album, artist, title, url, quote, read, optional note), in the shape `tools/check_evidence.py` reads.
- `unverified.json`: genre id → `alias` (title used → spelling on the page) and `unverified` (recalled or half-seen, not confirmed; fetch failures).
- `evidence-check.json`: this run's output of `tools/check_evidence.py` over `sources.json`.
- `notes/<genre id>.md`: each sub-agent's full notes: confidence, the list of every source address, neighbours with no id, and what fell short. The report below condenses them.
- `REPORT.md`: this file.

### Confidence, per genre
**`saidimusic`.** Medium confidence in the definition and the core of the playlist, low in the size and the start date. The definition rests on English Wikipedia (Music of Egypt, Tahtib, Mizmar), Real World Records' pages for the Musicians of the Nile (two-string rababa, "mizmar sa'idi" with circular breathing, the Metqal clan "seven generations") and the Naïade Productions press kit PDF (mizmar with tabla baladî for the tahtib stick dance, weddings and mawlids). Eleven songs are by the Musicians of the Nile and six by Metqal Qenawi and Shamandy Metqal, tied by performer pages. The rest are tied only by album or track titles that say "Saidi" or "mizmar sa'idi", and these are the weak ties: two belly-dance-market studio albums (Hollywood Music Center, 2000 and 2006), and late tracks by Funoon Shaabeya (2023) and El Rayes Mahrous El Sedfawy (℗ 1990). Metqal Qenawi's El Wady singles carry only a ℗ 1985 line. Shamandy Metqal is matched to the Shamandi Metqal on the Real World page by name only. No page dates the genre's start: the tree's 1700 rests on the seven generations and recordings from 1976. Sira hilaliya enters only through the Musicians of the Nile's own "Abou Zeïd el Hilali". No readable recordings were found for Sohar Magdy or Shamandy the singer, and the Ahmed Megahed release could not be tied to the Sa'idi singer.

**`lavani`.** Medium confidence in the songs and definition, low in the size. Every song is tied to lavani in a page's own words. 24 songs come from Aathavanitli Gani, a curated but fan-run Marathi lyrics archive whose song-type field reads लावणी. For 11 songs that archive is the only tie. Fourteen also have Wikipedia or Times of India support, for example the Sulochana Chavan page "known for her lavanis". The list is film and gramophone lavani from 1947 to 2022. There is nothing from working tamasha troupes and nothing by Yamunabai Waikar. Only Shobha Gurtu's two songs stand for the seated (baithakichi) style. The definition rests on a thin English Wikipedia page with "citation needed" tags; only its sourced paragraphs were used. The two `filmi` near-misses (Chikni Chameli, Dhagala Lagali Kala) are weaker than asked: no page calls a Hindi film song a lavani. Four songs have no year, and iTunes album dates may be digital-store dates. The sub-agent flags that the tree record gives `tradition: punjabi` for a Marathi genre (see Questions).

**`maloya`.** Medium-to-good confidence in the songs and definition, low in the size. Every track and album comes from MusicBrainz JSON. Thirteen songs are tied by a title naming maloya (Firmin Viry's Ocora album and 1976 PCR record, the 1976 compilation Peuple du Maloya, Bourbon Maloya, Lo Rwa Kaf, Gramoun Bébé, Lindigo's "Maloya Power"/"Oyé Maloya" and others). Twelve are tied by French or English Wikipedia calling the performer a maloya musician (Viry, Granmoun Lélé, Danyèl Waro, Ziskakan, Lindigo, Zanmari Baré). Salem Tradition is tied only through Christine Salem. The definition rests on English and French Wikipedia (kayamb, roulèr, bobre; call and response; ban until 1981; electric maloya from the late 1970s). Weak points: Ziskakan's "Bato fou" is band-pop, and Alain Péters stands in for electric maloya. Two years conflict between MusicBrainz and French Wikipedia (Soleye 1991/1995, Foutan Fonnker 2000/1999); the playlist uses MusicBrainz. The two Bourbon Maloya tracks have no year, nothing before 1976 has a track list, and Gramoun Sello was not found.

**`rara`.** Medium confidence in the songs and definition, low in the size. Tracks were read from the iTunes lookup API (Caribbean Revels, Soul Jazz's Rara in Haiti), MusicBrainz, the University of Wisconsin library record of the Alan Lomax Haiti box (Volume 4, "Rara: vodou in motion") and two clean Folkways liner PDFs (FE 4531, 1978; FE 4432, 1952). Each track is tied by album title, by band name (Bann Rara …) or by liner notes calling it a rara song. The definition rests on English, Haitian Creole and Spanish Wikipedia and Verna Gillis's FE 4531 notes. Weak points: the years are release years, while the recordings are much older (Lomax 1936–37 carries 2009). Soul Jazz's recordist is unknown. Caribbean Revels tracks are named after places and bands, not songs. Two Courlander tracks credit an unidentified band recorded by Marshall Stearns, and that artist string was written by the sub-agent. Dominican gagá rests on five Gillis field recordings only. Kònè could not be quoted because the SFW 40402 liner PDF is bad OCR.

**`huapango`.** Medium confidence in the songs and definition, low in the size. Every track and album comes from iTunes lookup or MusicBrainz JSON. The definition rests on English and Spanish Wikipedia (Huapango, Son huasteco): violin, jarana huasteca and huapanguera; falsetto; improvised coplas; the window in three dated layers (18th century per the English infobox, end of the 19th century in its text, the trio's present form from the 1950s per Spanish Wikipedia). Most songs are tied by album titles naming son huasteco or huapangos (Los Camperos de Valles, Los Caimanes, Trío Xoxocapa, El Gusto – 40 Años de Son Huasteco, Ocora's Danses et huapangos). Weak ties: Trío Chicontepec's albums name only "La Huasteca" or the Huastecan violin, and the 1976 album is dated on a 2025 reissue page. Los Brujos de Huejutla's track is on a mixed jarocho/huasteco festival album and is tied by Spanish Wikipedia naming them. The norteño near-miss rests on its album title only, and El Pastor's 1984 is a compilation year. Nothing before 1966 was verified. La Petenera appears three times by different trios; this is deliberate for a shared repertoire, but the lead may want to thin it.

**`verbunkos`.** Medium-to-high confidence in the definition, medium in the playlist, low in the size. The definition rests on English and Hungarian Wikipedia (Verbunkos, János Bihari, Csárdás): lassú and friss, dotted rhythms, the running-note friss, augmented seconds, violins and cimbalom, the 18th-century origin and recruiting up to 1848/49. All 23 songs come from MusicBrainz release JSON, and each is tied only by its own track title naming verbunk, verbunkos or körverbunk. That tie is honest but shallow, because no liner note was read. The playlist covers the Romani band tradition (Hungarian State Folk Ensemble 1955, Lakatos and Boross 1950s, Kálmán Balogh, Tcha Limberger), period-instrument Capella Savaria, and village verbunk. Six of the village tracks are by táncház bands (Sebő, Muzsikás, Hegedős, Csík) and overlap `tanchaz`. Bihari-, Lavotta- and Csermák-era pieces whose titles do not say verbunk are in unverified, so the art-verbunkos repertoire is under-represented. Five years are null (the Frémeaux 1954–59 reissue tracks, Capella Savaria). The Bartók Contrasts near-miss went to `modernism`, which exists and fits a 1938 work.

**`morenadaanddiablad`.** Medium confidence in the definition and songs, low in the size. The definition rests on English and Spanish Wikipedia (Morenada, Diablada, Carnival of Oruro): matraca, brass, "ritmo lento y paso cansino", c. 1790 per Thola, scores from the 1860s, the first organised diablada in 1904, Puno brass diablada from 1962. The tree's start of 1800 fits. Sixteen songs are brass-band recordings: Banda Espectacular Pagador (four albums, including a 1964 "Popurrí de Morenadas"), the Puno bands, Intercontinental Poopó, Brass Unbound and an Oruro field recording. Nine are folk-group pieces whose title names them morenada or diablada (Inti-Illimani, Illapu, Kjarkas, Jaime Torres, Zulma Yugar and others). Every tie is a track or album title. No prose page about any band was found, and Banda Pagador has no Wikipedia article. The Brass Unbound credit "Banda Espectular Parador" is read as Pagador. Several years are compilation years (Kjarkas 2006, Azoguini 2019), and the charango and piano "La diablada" versions are tied by title only. Northern Chile has no tracks, and both near-misses are `andean` huaynos because caporales and saya have no id.

**`sevillanas`.** Medium-high confidence in the definition and playlist, low in the size. The definition rests on English and Spanish Wikipedia (Sevillanas, Feria de Abril, and the pages of Los Romeros de la Puebla, Los Marismeños and Manuel Pareja Obregón). It covers the seguidilla roots, four sevillanas in triple time, guitar, palmas and castanets, rociera flute and tamboril, choral harmony and the later bass and orchestra. The two Wikipedias disagree on the metre (6/8 against 3/4). Thirteen songs come from MusicBrainz release JSON whose album or track title names sevillanas, from the 1925 Odeon "Sevillanas números 3 y 4" to Saura's 1992 film soundtrack. Two come from iTunes (Las Corraleras de Lebrija; Rocío Jurado) and the rest from Wikipedia. Weak points: Los del Río have one song, from a 2019 reissue of a 1990 album. Los Romeros de la Puebla appear only through their medley on the Saura soundtrack, because MusicBrainz kept answering "busy". Five songs have no year because they come from later compilations (Toronjo, Cantores de Híspalis, the Sevillanas de los Cuarenta copla singers). Rocío Jurado's iTunes year of 1962 was judged implausible and left null. Martirio's "He visto color" is an ironic, modern take at the edge of the genre. The size (15000–60000) is the largest of the batch, judged from the yearly album output of the Feria; it is not a count.

## What fell short
- **Genre ties by title.** In this batch the tie is more often a title than prose. Verbunkos and morenada/diablada are tied entirely by track or album titles. Sa'idi is partly tied that way (belly-dance-market albums), and so is lavani, where 11 songs rest on one archive. Liner notes and archive catalogues that would make the ties stronger were not read: Hungaroton and Fonó booklets, any page on the Pagador and Poopó bands, and Smithsonian Folkways SFW 40512 and SFW 40402, whose PDFs are bad OCR.
- **Missing layers.** Lavani has no tamasha-troupe or Yamunabai Waikar recordings. Huapango has nothing verified before 1966, maloya nothing before 1976 and morenada nothing before 1964. Northern Chile has no diablada tracks. Dominican gagá has five field tracks. Gramoun Sello (maloya), Sohar Magdy and Shamandy (Sa'idi), and Niña de los Peines's sevillanas were not found on readable pages. MusicBrainz "busy" answers stopped the sevillanas research on Los Romeros de la Puebla's own albums, María del Monte and Salmarina, and the maloya research on Alain Péters's *Rest' la Maloya*. None of these was retried in a loop.
- **Years.** Some years are null: sevillanas 5, verbunkos 5, lavani 4, maloya 2, plus several in Sa'idi and morenada. Many years are release or reissue years rather than recording years, and two maloya years conflict between sources. Details are in each `notes/<id>.md`, section (d).
- **Start dates.** No page dates the start of Sa'idi music (tree 1700) or gives a year for maloya (tree 1750; the source says 18th century during slavery).
- **Duplicates.** Huapango repeats La Petenera three times and Cielito lindo, El gusto and La huasanga twice each, by different trios.
- **Playability.** Streaming availability was not checked for any genre.

2. Six verbunkos tracks are village verbunks played by táncház revival bands. Should they sit in `verbunkos`, `tanchaz` or both?

   - Lavani: tamasha, powada, gavlan, Marathi film song and bhavgeet, natya sangeet.
   - Maloya: electric and electronic maloya, maloggae, servis kabaré.
   - Rara: rara tech, gagá as its own genre, kanaval bands, Vodou drumming.
   - Huapango: huapango arribeño, son costumbre.
   - Verbunkos: csárdás, the Moravian and Slovak verbuňk, legényes, palotás.
   - Morenada and diablada: caporales, saya afroboliviana, tinku and sikuri, diablada pillareña.
   - Sevillanas: Spanish copla, fandangos de Huelva, seguidillas, coros rocieros.
