# shokaandgunka: notes

## (a) Confidence
**`shokaandgunka`.** Medium-to-good confidence in the definition and the song list, low in the size. All 111 claims are `found`. Every recording was read from the raw iTunes Japan lookup JSON. Every song is tied to the genre by its own Japanese Wikipedia page calling it a 唱歌, 文部省唱歌 or 軍歌, with three exceptions:
- **ふじの山 and 朧月夜** are tied only by the 文部省唱歌 page naming them among the best-known Monbushō songs.
- **暁に祈る** is called a 戦時歌謡 (wartime popular song) in the page's lead. Only a section heading calls it a 軍歌, so it is the weakest gunka tie.
- **愛国行進曲** is called a national patriotic song and the "de facto second national anthem", not a gunka. It also sits on the Columbia album titled 軍歌・愛国歌・戦時歌謡.

The definition rests on English Wikipedia (Gunka: Western brass instruments, four-four time, major key, taught in schools until 1945, banned under the Occupation) and Japanese Wikipedia (軍歌, 唱歌 (教科), 小学唱歌集, 文部省唱歌). The 小学唱歌集 page also backs both parentage lines: 12 to 22 of its 91 songs were hymns, and the 軍艦 song became the navy's official band march. The tree's 1881 matches the first volume's date, 明治14年11月24日.

**Weak ties and gaps:**
- Most Meiji and Taishō songs are heard in post-war choir and Columbia studio re-recordings. Their release years are null wherever iTunes shows only a compilation date.
- Only the six Columbia 78s of 1937–43 (from the オリジナルSP復刻 reissue) are period recordings. Of these, 露営の歌, 燃ゆる大空 and 若鷲の歌 are dated by their Wikipedia pages, which name the same singers.
- 夏は来ぬ is dated 1900 by its page, where an 1896 date was recalled.
- Many song pages carry "sources needed" banners.

The size is a judgement from songbook and wartime output counts, not a count of recordings.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Dōyō (童謡), the children's art-song movement from 1918 (Akai tori).** Examples: 赤とんぼ, 七つの子. It is the natural neighbour of school shōka, and many compilations mix the two as 童謡・唱歌.
- **Wartime popular song (戦時歌謡 / 軍国歌謡), 1931–45.** Examples: 麦と兵隊, 暁に祈る, 湖上の尺八. It sits between gunka and kayōkyoku. Here it was split: clear gunka stay in, and the sentimental songs go to `kayokyoku`.
- **Dormitory songs (寮歌) of the old higher schools.** Example: 嗚呼玉杯に花うけて.
- **Self-Defense Force unit songs (隊歌).**

## (d) What fell short
- No Meiji or Taishō period recording was found on a readable page. The earliest takes are the 1937–43 Columbia 78s.
- 蛍の光, 春が来た, 紅葉, 村祭 and 鯉のぼり are left out. Their Japanese Wikipedia pages were not found by direct address, and the Wikipedia search API refused the script (rate limit).
- No `changga` near-miss: Korean Wikipedia has no 학도가 page, and no recording was read.
- Release years are null for 15 of 26 songs, because iTunes gives only compilation or reissue dates.
- 暁に祈る and 愛国行進曲 have the softest gunka ties (see unverified.json).
- Playability on streaming services was not checked beyond the iTunes Japan catalogue.
