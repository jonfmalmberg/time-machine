# funana: notes

## (a) Confidence

Medium-to-good confidence in the definition and the songs, low in the size. All 80 claims are `found`. The definition rests on English and Portuguese Wikipedia (gaita and ferrinho, diatonic scales, accordion solos between verse and refrain, two-beat pulse from andante to vivace, couple dance with knee flexes, the oral-tradition origin at the start of the 20th century, the ban in the capital, Bulimundo's electric revival in the 1980s, Ferro Gaita's return to the gaita) and on Ostinato Records' Bandcamp text for Pour Me a Grog (Ferro Gaita's 1997 disc "based on the gaita, ferrinho and bass guitar"). Track existence comes from the iTunes Portugal lookup API and one MusicBrainz release (Bitori and Chando Graciosa, 1998). The ties to the genre:
- **A page lists the song as a funaná example.** Portuguese Wikipedia's "Exemplos de funanás" covers Sema Lopi, Djonsinho Cabral, Moças di Mangui, Fomi 47, Kortel di Rabidanti, Matrialistas, Codjeta and Nôs Cultura (8 songs).
- **The album or track title names the genre.** This covers Bitori's three tracks (reissued as *Legend of Funaná*), Febrei di Funaná, Pombinha Mansa (on *Rei Di Funaná*), Funaná House, Bejo Bafatada (*Rei di Funaná*), Nha Balila (*Más Um Funaná*), the five *Pour Me a Grog: The Funaná Revolt* tracks and Nôs Funaná (14 songs). These are ties by title only.
- **A performer page calls them funaná artists.** Bulimundo's Compasso Pilon (Portuguese Wikipedia) and Codé di Dona's Djan-Bai (Portuguese Wikipedia), 2 songs.

**Weak ties and gaps:**
- Four *Pour Me a Grog* tracks (Mulato Ferrera, Pom Um Grogu, Tra Tchapéu, Mô Na Máma) have no original year, and so do Codjeta (sources disagree: 1992 or 1995) and Djan-Bai (store reissue ℗ 2014).
- Pombinha Mansa is Zeca di Nha Reinalda's later recording (℗ 2014), not Bulimundo's 1981 one, which could not be read.
- Dino d'Santiago's Nôs Funaná (2018) is a modern pop take, tied by its title only.
- Simentera is a roots band that also plays morna and coladeira; Codjeta rests on the Wikipedia example list alone.
- Four Wikipedia row quotes put a space where the page has a line break.

The size is a judgement from store listings and Wikipedia's history, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Batuku (batuque)**: Santiago's women's drumming and song genre, the closest relative. Examples: Ntóni Denti d'Oro and Nha Mita Pereira, *Cape Verde: Batuque & Finaçon*. It has no id, so it was not used as a near-miss.
- **Tabanka**: Santiago's processional music. Ferro Gaita's "Rei Di Tabanka" was kept out for this reason.
- **Finaçon (the genre)**: improvised sung verse, as opposed to the band of the same name.
- **Funaná-kizomba and electro-funaná dance hybrids**: Grupo Pilon, and label compilations such as *Funaná & Kizomba 3*. Kizomba has an id, but these are mixes, not near-misses of either genre.

## (d) What fell short
- MusicBrainz was busy three times for Bulimundo's *Batuco* (1981), so "Sant' Antoni la Belêm" and Bulimundo's "Pombinha Mansa" are missing.
- No readable track list was found for:
  - Finaçon's 1990 album *Funaná* ("Si manera")
  - Codé di Dona's 1996 and 1998 albums ("Pomba")
  - Eduíno e Petcha
  - Zezé di Nha Reinalda's "Djentis d’ aságua"
- Nothing earlier than 1978 is on record here. The first recordings of gaita players before independence were not found.
- Playability on streaming services was not checked beyond the presence of an iTunes store entry.
- Original years for compilation tracks are missing.
