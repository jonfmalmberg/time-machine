# changga: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`changga`.** I am fairly sure of the definition and much less sure of the playlist. The definition rests on the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture (entries 창가, 신식 창가, 유행 창가, 계몽가요, 학도가, 독립군가), Korean Wikipedia (창가, 한국의 가곡) and English Wikipedia (K-pop, Music of South Korea, Trot). All 64 claims are `found`. The weakness is the recordings: changga were a sung repertoire of schools, churches and rallies, and nearly all of it predates Korean commercial recording. No 1920s 78 rpm original could be read on a script-readable page, so all 13 tracks are later recordings (1977–2019) of changga songs, mostly by trot-era singers on nostalgia anthologies and on a 1988 independence-song album. Every song is tied to the genre **by the song, not the performer**: a page names the song itself as a changga, a changga-songbook song or the first yuhaeng changga (학도가: Encyclopedia of Korean Culture and English Wikipedia Trot; 희망가: English Wikipedia K-pop; 사의 찬미: English Wikipedia Yun Sim-deok and Trot; 독립군가 and 소년행진가: the Encyclopedia's 계몽가요 entry, which gives 계몽창가 and 애국창가 as other names). The recordings themselves were checked as existing through iTunes lookup records. Weak ties:
- **소년행진가** is tied by title only. The album does not credit 안창호 as lyricist.
- **Arrangements are not checked.** The trot singers' versions may be in a later, trot-flavoured style. 한영애's 2003 version is a modern reinterpretation that only nods to the period.
- **Three songs carry 12 of the 13 tracks.** The list is narrow.
- **사의 찬미** sits on the boundary with yuhaengga and trot. It is included because two pages call it the first yuhaeng changga.

The size is a judgement, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Korean art song (가곡, gagok in the modern sense).** Hong Nan-pa's 봉선화 (1920) grew out of changga, according to Korean Wikipedia 한국의 가곡. There is no id for it; Russian romance and kundiman are not stretched to cover it.
- **Korean children's song (동요).** Examples are 반달 and 따오기 from the 1920s. The Encyclopedia names creative children's songs as an offspring of changga.
- **Independence-army and Liberation-army songs (독립군가, 광복군가).** These are a 1910s–1940s military-song repertoire. They overlap changga only where the Encyclopedia names them among the 계몽창가.
- **Manyo (만요).** The comic songs of the 1930s, named in English Wikipedia Trot. They come after changga.
- **Sinminyo (신민요).** This sits closer to `trot` and `pungmul`; it is not a changga neighbour.

The near ids used are `trot` (이애리수, 황성의 적) and `tongguitarandnorae` (한대수's folk-rock 희망가).

## (d) What fell short
- **No period recordings.** No 1920s original was found on a page a script can read: Yun Sim-deok's 1926 사의 찬미, Chae Gyu-yeop's 학도가 and 희망가, and Park Chae-seon and Lee Ryu-saek's 1925 이 풍진 세월. MusicBrainz has nothing for these songs before 1975. The iTunes Korean storefront returned empty results, so the US storefront was used. The Korean Sound Archive (한국음반아카이브) or a reissue CD's liner notes could supply real 78s.
- **Status is `thin`.** There are 13 tracks drawn from three main songs, plus two patriotic songs.
- **1916 Hawaii Honolulu Korean Anthem** (Incheon Concert Chamber, 2022) is likely a changga songbook set to music. No page ties it to one, so it was left out.
- **Hangul names not read for four artists.** Choi Jung Ja, Nam Il-hae, Hwang Geum Sim and Jeonghee keep their iTunes spellings.
- **Start dates conflict.** The Encyclopedia gives 1876 in its summary and 1896 in its body for 황제탄신경축가.
- **Not researched:** 권학가 and 거국가 recordings (none found on iTunes), 애국가 sung to Auld Lang Syne, and 경부철도가 recordings (none found).
