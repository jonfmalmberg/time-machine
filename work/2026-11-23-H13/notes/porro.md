# porro: notes

## (a) Confidence

**`porro`.** Fairly sure of the songs and the definition, unsure of the size and of many years. All 62 claims are `found`. The definition rests on Spanish Wikipedia's Porro (música) (Córdoba, Sucre and Bolívar; gaita groups meeting military wind bands; 2/2 time; bombardino-clarinet dialogue; the porro and bozá sections; palitiao and tapao; the word in use from the late 19th century; the orchestral spread 1940s-1970s; the San Pelayo festival) and English Wikipedia's Porro (brass bands or orchestras, couple dance, Sinú origin, the rim stroke). Song ties are of three kinds:
- **The album or track title names the genre (title-only ties).** Historia musical del porro pelayero (María Varilla, La mona Carolina, Roque Guzmán, Soy pelayero, Sara Esther); Pedro Laza's Porro! (El catabre); Porrovia… Los 40 Porros del Siglo (12 tracks); the tracks labelled "(Porro)" on Porros, Cumbias y Gaitas (5 tracks); "Borrachera, Porro"; "Porro Viejo Pelayero"; Adriana Lucía's "Porro nuevo". Of these, María Varilla, La mona Carolina, Roque Guzmán and Soy pelayero are also named as porros in the text of Spanish Wikipedia. Rufo Garrido, Clímaco Sarmiento, Alfonso Piña, Los Corraleros de Majagual, Orquesta Manuel J. Posada, Jesús Nuncira Machado, Orquesta Sonoritmo, La Combo d'Lido, Ramón Ropaín and the Cereteana, Bajera and Manguelito bands rest on the title tie alone (the English Wikipedia list names Sonoritmo, Manuel J. Posada, Nuncira Machado and La Combo d'Lido, but that section is unreferenced and was not quoted).
- **A performer page calls them porro players.** Pedro Laza (es.wikipedia: "en el ámbito del porro y fandango"), Lucho Bermúdez ("como la cumbia y el porro"), and Pacho Galán, Edmundo Arias and La Sonora Cordobesa (listed in the Porro article among the orchestras that spread it), plus the Banda 19 de Marzo de Laguneta (named there as one of the most popular bands).

**Weak ties:** Porrovia is a compilation title and may include pieces a purist would call gaita or fandango; Sara Esther is tied only by the anthology's title; Adriana Lucía's Porro nuevo is a pop-leaning revival. Many years are null because iTunes reissue dates are not original dates; the years used are iTunes track release dates that look original (1956-1974), not separately checked claims. The size is a judgement, not a count.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Fandango (Colombian band fandango)** of the same bandas pelayeras: e.g. "Fandango viejo", Banda Bajera de San Pelayo, on Historia musical del porro pelayero. The closest neighbour of all; no id.
- **Orchestral gaita (gaita colombiana as played by orchestras)**: e.g. Lucho Bermúdez "Gaiteando", Edmundo Arias "Ligia (Gaita)" on Porros, Cumbias y Gaitas. `gaitazuliana` is Venezuelan Christmas gaita and was not stretched.
- **Mapalé**: e.g. Lucho Bermúdez "Prende la vela" (labelled "Mapalé" on the Ropaín album). No id.
- **Merecumbé** (associated with Pacho Galán; recalled, not looked up). No id.
- **Puya** (named alongside porro and fandango on Spanish Wikipedia) has no id of its own; it sits under vallenato.

## (d) What fell short
- Lucho Bermúdez's own famous porros (Carmen de Bolívar, Salsipuedes, San Fernando, Colombia tierra querida) are on readable track lists, but no page read labels those recordings as porros, so only Tolú (via Porrovia) represents his own orchestra.
- Pachito E'ché, Se va el caimán and Me voy pa' Cataca (named on English Wikipedia) were not found as readable recordings in this run.
- Original release years for most orchestral tracks are unknown; Discos Fuentes reissues carry reissue dates.
- The Spanish Wikipedia pages for the song María Varilla and for Alejandro Ramírez Ayazo returned 404, and the Wikipedia search API was rate-limited, so song articles were not searched.
- The El Universal list of the 100 best porros (2014) was not read; it would be the natural source for the canon and the core figure.
