# sudanesehaqiba: notes

## (a) Confidence
Medium confidence in the definition and in the songs being haqiba, low in the years and the size. All 85 claims are `found`. The definition rests on English Wikipedia (Music of Sudan, Khalil Farah, Abdel Karim Karouma, Ibrahim al Kashif) and Arabic Wikipedia (موسيقى سودانية, خليل فرح, الأمين برهان, محمد بشير عتيق, إبراهيم الكاشف, كمال ترباس): lead singer and chorus, tar/riq frame drum and handclaps, madeeh roots, weddings, pentatonic melody, the 1940s naming after Radio Omdurman's Hageebat al-Fann, violin and accordion added by Sarour on late-1920s records. The 1920 start fits the pages ("from the 1920s onward"). Songs are tied in three ways: (1) performer pages: Karouma (English Wikipedia), Sarour (Music of Sudan: "a pioneer of hageeba"), Badi Mohamed al-Tayeb and Mubarak Hassan Barakat (Arabic Wikipedia); Khalil Farah as writer of "عزة في هواك"; (2) album titles naming the genre, which is the only tie for Mirghani al-Ma'mun and Ahmed Hassan Gumaa, Awad al-Karim Abdallah, Kamal Tarbas, Obeid al-Tayeb, Khidir Bashir, Awlad al-Mawrada, Abdel Aziz Mohamed Daoud ("زهر الرياض"), Insaf Fathi and Asim Albanna (title only); (3) Karouma's "مسوا نوركم" is also confirmed as an Odéon 78 by the BnF catalogue. Weak ties: almost every track comes from one rights-holder's 2024 digital reissues (Nile Melodies Archive) with no recording dates, so 20 of 22 years are null; the Sarour singles sit under an uploader account with odd 2012–2013 dates; Insaf Fathi's track title is in Latin on the page. Both near-misses are Ibrahim al-Kashif, because the natural second neighbour (madeeh) has no id.

## (c) Neighbours with no id in the tree
- **Sudanese madeeh (madih nabawi)**: the Sufi praise singing with tar drums that haqiba took its melodies from. It is the closest neighbour and the parent the pages name; the tree has only `tilawa` (recitation) and no madeeh id. Not stretched.
- **Tum tum (women's rhythmic choral song from Kosti, 1930s)**, named on English Wikipedia's Music of Sudan as growing out of praise singing.
- **Aghani al-banat (women's wedding songs with daluka drum)**: not researched.
- Post-haqiba modern song has an id, `sudanesepopularson`, used for both near-misses.

## (d) What fell short
- **Years**: the Nile Melodies Archive reissues (almost every song) carry only a 2024 digital date, so 20 of 22 playlist years are null. A Radio Omdurman or 78 discography with dates would fix this.
- **MusicBrainz**: searches for haqiba spellings answered "busy" or returned 0 releases; nothing was used from it beyond finding the Two Niles release.
- **Gallica** refused scripts (Cloudflare), so Karouma's 78 is known from the BnF catalogue record, not the audio page.
- **Wikipedia API** rate-limited heavily; pages for Mirghani al-Ma'mun, Awad al-Karim Abdallah, Abdel Aziz Mohamed Daoud and Mohamed Ahmed Sarour do not exist under the titles tried (404), so those singers rest on album titles or English Wikipedia.
- **Not found on readable pages**: recordings by Al-Amin Burhan, Wad El Faki, Ibrahim Abdel Jalil, Zinjar, and Khalil Farah singing his own songs.
- **The size** is a judgement, not a count.
