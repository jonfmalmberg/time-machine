# Genres the helpers named that the tree lacks: the lead's verdicts

Dated 23 November 2026 (Pacific). Source: the "neighbours with no id" lists in `work/2026-11-23-H12/`, `H13/` and `H14/REPORT.md`.
Basis: the lead's general knowledge of each style, not sources. A verdict decides where the item goes on the roadmap; anything added still needs sourced records. Decision rule: `DECISIONS.md` D10 and principle 27.

## Add as a genre, in order of value
| # | Genre | Why |
| -: | :- | :- |
| 1 | Copla / canción española (Spain, 1930s to 1960s) | The mainstream Spanish popular song of two generations. The tree's `iberianpop` names it but ends in 1800: fix that record or add this. A Spaniard over 60 would miss it at once. |
| 2 | Dōyō (Japanese children's song, 1918 onward) | Known to every Japanese person; separate in purpose and sound from shōka. |
| 3 | Música guajira / punto cubano (Cuban country song) | A real gap beside `son`; the source of twoubadou's guitar style. |
| 4 | Nanguan / nanyin (Fujian, Taiwan) | One of the oldest living Chinese ensemble traditions, distinct language and sound. |
| 5 | Modern Amazigh band music (Izenzaren, Ousmane; Morocco, 1970s onward) | The popular child of rways and ahwash; widely heard. |
| 6 | Batuku, with finaçon and tabanka as sub-genres (Cape Verde) | Older and distinct from funaná; the island's other root music. |
| 7 | Ecuadorian bolero and rockola | What most Ecuadorians of the 1960s to 1980s listened to; pasillo alone under-represents the country. |
| 8 | Sudanese madeeh (praise song) | Widely heard, distinct from haqiba. |
| 9 | Korean children's song (dongyo) | As dōyō, for Korea. Lower because changga and minyo cover part of it. |
| 10 | Parsi theatre music | Historic source of nurthi and of early Hindi film song. Few recordings: low. |
| 11 | Rondalla (Philippine string band) | Real and distinct, small listening audience. |
| 12 | Trinidad parang | Seasonal, real, small. |
