# What the app is for, as people should meet it

Recorded 23 November 2026 (evening, Pacific), from the owner's message after the first friends tried the live site. A record of intent: not rewritten, corrected by dated lines.

## What the owner said
- The walkthrough that gives a new user their "aha" moment is not built, and it matters.
- Some users think the app "just creates a prompt": a generic one with no research or direction behind it, as if it only wrote "make me a playlist, Canada in the 80s".
- They are also not aware that Spotify is only one of the ways to listen.
- "We want to market the app as a way to explore the world of music and find new sounds they love and would otherwise never hear. 'Spotify prompt generator' is not the right vibe to shoot for, although it may be true one of its good features."

## What follows from it (the lead's reading)
- The app's worth is the research: which songs people in a place actually heard in a year, which sounds were alive there, where each sound came from. A new visitor must see that research before they see any export.
- A prompt is one way out of the app, beside others. It should never be the first or the largest thing on the screen.
- The first visit is judged by whether the person hears something they would not have found, not by whether they understood the controls.
- This sharpens principle 3 ("a moment, fast") and roadmap milestone 1; it does not replace the founding test (a local or an old person from the place feels nostalgia).
